Background. Debate's Claims now works the way you chose: a list of the article's main claims, and you tick some (or type one of your own) and press Check to search the web for them. When you share an article publicly, a visitor sees Reception, the list of claims, and the old automatic search's claims if there was one. They do not see the checks you ran. A check can hold a claim you typed in your own words, so as built your checks stay yours.

A. Leave it as built. Visitors see the list of claims, but none of the sources your checks found. Costs nothing. A visitor to a shared paper sees less than an owner who has checked a few claims.

B. Show a visitor the checks of the article's own listed claims, never a claim you typed. The sources appear under each listed claim for them, read-only, as they do for you. About half a day: one more field through the public page, the same address filtering every other Debate row gets, and tests that a typed claim never crosses. Your checks become part of what you publish when you share.

What would decide it: whether you think of a check as your own working (A) or as part of the article's page once shared (B).

Recommended: B. The listed claims are the article's own words and the sources are public pages, so nothing private crosses, and a shared paper is more useful with them.
