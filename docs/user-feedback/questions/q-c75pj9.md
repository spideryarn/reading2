When someone adds an article that is already public and picks "Read the public copy (free)", should they be able to leave their own notes and comments on it, or is the free copy read-only?

A. Read-only, as built today. The free copy shows everything already made for it; for notes, chat or search of your own you add your own copy, which uses one article from your allowance. Costs nothing more. Gives up: a reader who only wants to jot a few notes has to spend an article.

B. Free copy plus your own private notes. Your comments and bookmarks on someone else's public article, visible only to you. Gives the free option real value. Costs: a new kind of request (signed in, not the owner, writing something) that the security rules do not have today, and somewhere new to keep the notes. A plan of its own, with your sign-off on the security change.

Recommendation: A for now. The offer is built and works either way, and B is worth doing as its own piece once readers ask for it.

Details

What you asked (spya-ahvk74, this morning): when someone adds an article we already have as public, ask whether they would rather read the public one for free or have their own copy, which uses one of their slots.

What was built: the add page now notices, before anything is charged, that someone else has made the same address public, and stops with two buttons. "Read the public copy (free)" opens that article. "Add my own copy" carries on as an ordinary import and uses one article. The link hover card and the MCP tool offer the same two.

Why this is a question: today a signed-in reader on someone else's public article is treated exactly like a stranger who is not signed in. They can read everything already made (summaries, glossary, the owner's saved searches and so on) but cannot comment, bookmark, chat or search. So "free" currently means "free, without notes of your own". The screen says so in plain words.

What B would involve: the server today knows two kinds of request, the owner's (may do anything) and anyone's (read only, never spends). B adds a third, "signed in, not the owner, writing their own notes", which touches the security map's defences, so it is not something to slip in unattended. The earlier write-up of the same idea for AI on a public article is plan 261006k.

What would decide it: whether readers who choose the free copy then want to annotate it. If they mostly just read, A is enough.
