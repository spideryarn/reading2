Should the literature search in Sources (the part that puts findings beside passages in the margin) start by itself sooner than it does now?

A. When you press the margin button on (recommended). The first press of the margin button on an article starts the search if it has never run. A reload, a pasted link, or coming back to an article that already had the margin on does not. About 30 to 50 cents per article you do this on, the findings arriving in the margin a minute or so later.

B. Whenever the margin is showing, including when you just arrive at an article with it already on. More articles get findings without your doing anything, but a reload or a restored view can spend.

C. On every new article, on import. Findings are there the first time you open it, but it is about 30 to 50 cents more on every import, about a third more than an import costs today, including articles you never open.

D. Leave it as it is: the search starts when you press into Sources' Where it sits.

Recommended: A. It is the press that says "I want the margin for this one", it spends nothing on articles you never look at that way, and it is a small change.

Details

What you asked, this morning (28 Oct), from the margin on LeCun's paper: "I would just like to lean into that. So I think ideally sources would run more proactively, require fewer options from the user, and populate the marginalia mode more."

What the margin gets from Sources today. Since 26 Oct the literature search's findings (what later papers challenged, what came since, a claim that may need a citation) sit in the margin beside the passages they are about, as shut lines you open. That is the part of Sources that is anchored to passages, so it is the part that can fill the margin. The search reads the whole article, plans a dozen web searches, and writes findings tied to the article's own words.

Why this is a question and not just done. On 25 Oct you set the rule: free things proactively, things that cost money only when you open Sources, and very costly things only on request. The search is the costliest thing in Sources (about 30 cents at standard power, about 52 under high-powered AI; the ledger's three real runs average 47 cents). Options A to C each loosen that rule by a different amount.

What a press means here. The app already treats "you pressed something" differently from "you arrived somewhere", so a reload or a pasted link never spends. A keeps to that; B does not.

"Fewer options from the user." Partly done on 25 and 26 Oct: Sources became three chips, and opening Where it sits starts the search. Two buttons still ask: Reception's "Search the web" (what others have written about the piece) and Claims' "List its claims". And checking a claim does not yet put anything in the margin. These are the next candidates if you want them folded into one automatic run; I have not touched them. Say if you want them in the same answer.

Nothing has been built for this yet. The other half of your report, explanations of confusing passages, is the separate question q-gkrkf5.
