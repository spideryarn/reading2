Opening an article you archived now puts it back on your shelf, as you asked. Should that also put a public article you archived back on the public list (/read/public)?

A. Yes, leave it as built (recommended). Opening undoes archiving everywhere, which is one simple rule. It costs nothing. The price: if you archived a public article to take it off the public list, opening it puts it back there, and you would archive it again.

B. No. Opening puts it back on your own shelf but leaves the public list alone. That needs a separate switch, so a little more code and one more thing to remember. It gives you a way to keep an article off the public list while still reading it.

Recommended: A, unless you archive public articles specifically to hide them from strangers. If you do, B.

Details

What you reported (30 Oct, #743): you opened The Bat (nagel-bat) and it was not on your shelf. It had been archived since 11 October, and opening it did not change that. The session fixed it, and the fix is on dev, to go out in the next deploy: opening an article you own now un-archives it.

Two consequences it flagged. First, if you archive from the button at the top of the reading view and then reload, the article comes back, because the reload counts as opening it. Second, the one this question is about: archiving is currently also how a public article comes off the public list, so opening it again republishes it there.
