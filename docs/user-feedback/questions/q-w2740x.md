Two calls about what the guide may do with your things. Answer like "1A 2A".

1. Saving why you're reading, and About you, from what you tell the guide.
A (recommended): the guide picks the reason out of your own message, word for word; the page checks the words really are yours and saves them by itself, with an Undo. About you gets a button showing the full text, which you press. No model-written words ever become your reason.
B: keep what was built today: a "Keep this as why you're reading" button under your first answer, which saves your whole message on your press. About you stays on the profile page.
C: the guide writes a short reason in its own words and offers it as a button to press.

2. Share, private link and archive from the guide ("do you want to create a private link for your journal club?").
A (recommended): the guide offers a button that takes you to the right control on the article's Metadata page (sharing, or Archive), and you do it there. It moves you; it never shares or archives anything itself.
B: buttons in the answer that do it on your press: create and copy a private link, make it public, archive.
C: not yet.

Details

Your report (2026-10-09, from the changelog page): the guide's "why are you reading" box was confusing, you wanted the chat itself to greet you from what it knows and ask why you're reading, to update your profile and reasons from what you say, and to help you take actions like a private link, sharing or archiving.

What is built and on dev (plan 261009i): the guide now greets you in the conversation, with no box. It welcomes you to the piece by title, asks why you are reading it, quotes the start of About you and asks if it is still right, and the guide's first real reply (which has your profile and the whole article) does the personal part. Under your first answer, "Keep this as why you're reading" saves your own words when you press it, and never overwrites a reason you saved elsewhere. The guide also takes Live now, and "Guide" in the command bar opens it.

Why these two were not built: both are a model's suggestion acting on your data, and the rule written into our security notes is that anything the AI proposes that writes your data, spends money or leaves the article is a button you press, never an act. 1A would be the first exception for a write; 2B would put sharing, the one action that changes who can read an article, one press away from a sentence an article's own text could have steered the model into writing. That is why the recommendation for 2 only moves you to the control.

What 1A costs: a new kind of guide output (a quoted span), a check that it is a substring of your message, an Undo line, and a one-line change to the security notes. It gives up nothing you have today; a planted instruction in an article could at worst choose which of your own words get saved, and Undo takes it back.

What 2A costs: one more kind of button, a move to a Metadata section, the same as the guide's existing "open a mode" buttons. 2B costs a write path per action and is the one I would not do without you.
