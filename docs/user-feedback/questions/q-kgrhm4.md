What this is about. When you open an article for the first time, it starts in Summary with Marginalia beside it (your choice of 2026-10-04). If you came from the add page without saying why you are reading, a "Why are you reading this?" box used to pop up.

As built now: in that one case (no reason given, and a window wide enough for a band) the article opens on the guide in the left band instead of the pop-up. The guide's greeting is written by us, costs nothing, and holds the same "why you're reading" box. Every other first open still starts in Summary. On a phone the pop-up stays.

A. Keep it as built. The guide appears unasked only when it has a question to ask.

B. Every first open starts on the guide instead of Summary, with Summary one press away. Most guidance for a new reader, but the article opens on a conversation rather than on a map of the piece. Free, since the greeting is ours.

C. As B, and when you already gave a reason, the guide speaks first: a model-written opening ("given you're after the method, start at section 3; here are two searches"). Costs a model call on every first open whether or not you read it, roughly 10 cents for a long paper on the default model, and a few seconds before it appears.

D. B or C only for new readers (no other article opened yet), Summary for everyone else.

What decides it: whether the first screen should orient the piece (Summary) or orient the reader (the guide), and whether 10 cents an article is worth an unprompted opening.

Recommendation: D with B's free greeting. New readers get the guide, experienced ones keep Summary, and nothing is spent unasked.
