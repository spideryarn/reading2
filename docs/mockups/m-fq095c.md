You said to try a few layouts, screenshot them and let some product-manager and first-time-reader subagents judge. These are the real Structure panel on a local copy of the paper you were reading (Consciousness in AI), with the reader in "Predictive Processing". A script edited the panel on the page, so the type, colours and widths are the real ones. The sub-section titles are the author's own headings; the one summary is made up, because no model has made the extra level yet (that waits for the 1 November key reset).

Today, for comparison: "Other Theories and Conditions" is one row.

X, chosen: the section you are in opens to show its sub-sections, indented; the one you are in is orange with its summary. Everything else is as today, and a section without sub-sections looks exactly as it does now.

image: m-fq095c-x-nested.png

Y: a third column, here in a 1,520px window. The band hardly gets wider on a wider window (about 700px either way), so each column is about 230px and every title wraps.

image: m-fq095c-y-third-column.png

Z: the columns slide down a level inside a section that has sub-sections. Column A becomes the part's sections, column B the sub-sections, and the parts shrink to a small "All 8 parts" link.

image: m-fq095c-z-slide.png

The judges saw these as X, Y and Z with no recommendation. All three picked X. The two first-time readers found Y "busy" and "too much to scan", and said Z hid the rest of the paper behind "a tiny grey link" ("I might think the paper is only these six sections"). The product manager ranked X, then Z, then Y: X reuses what exists and matches the phone list, Z reshuffles the columns every time you cross into or out of a section that has sub-sections, and Y needs a third layout plus a fallback for narrower windows. Their tweaks are built into X: a sub-section run too long for the column falls back to today's closed row with a count, the path from part to section to sub-section reads as one trail, and the section's summary moves into its hover card while the section is open.

Something both readers noticed that this change leaves alone: the panel numbers the sub-section 5.5.2 while the article's own heading says 2.4.2. Structure uses its own positional numbers, not the author's.
