# Talks

Where Greg could give a talk, and what kind, with a ranked list of venues near London.

Up: [marketing-overview.md](marketing-overview.md) · [vision.md](vision.md)

## What kind of talk

Greg presents well. A talk about ideas, with the product as the example, is easier to get invited
to; a straight demo suits a room that has already asked. Either is fine (Greg, 2026-10-09: *"Whatever
works"*). Which rooms to aim for:

> I was imagining more like a kind of product conference rather than an academic conference. Though
> an academic conference might be interesting, but … they're usually interested in their particular
> domain rather than mine. I suppose I could find one that's about AI or HCI or human augmentation
> via AI that would be ideal.
>
> — Greg, 2026-10-09

So: product conferences first, plus academic venues on AI, HCI or human augmentation, where the
subject itself is the domain. Within a few hours of London, over the next few months. A small room
where Greg can talk to people afterwards beats a big one, because the goal for now is conversations
with individual readers ([marketing-overview.md § What success means right now](marketing-overview.md#what-success-means-right-now)).
A recording of any talk can be reused afterwards.

## Venues

Researched 2026-10-09 by a web-search agent, ranked by how many real readers each puts Greg in
front of. **Dates were checked that day, and "unverified" means no 2026–27 date was found.** The
search was weak on Luma and Meetup calendars, so check the live page before acting on any of these.

**The short version.** The best-fitting room is Feeling of Computing London. After it come four
routes into rooms of people who read papers for a living, each reached by an email rather than a
call for talks: ReproducibiliTea, PhD training and library sessions, Advait Sarkar and the Cambridge
HCI seminars, and a session Greg hosts himself. AI demo nights are easy to get into, but their
audiences build things rather than read. Most conferences in the window have already closed their
calls.

### High fit

- **Feeling of Computing London** (was Future of Coding London) —
  [feelingof.com/london](https://feelingof.com/london/), calendar
  [luma.com/foclondon](https://luma.com/foclondon). Seven-minute demos at Newspeak House,
  Shoreditch, about monthly; the February 2026 edition had 102 people and a waitlist. The crowd is
  tools for thought, the closest thing in London to Spideryarn's readers. Slot: the demo form at
  feelingof.london/demo. Next date unverified.
  - Its **virtual meetup** ([luma.com/feelingofcomputing](https://luma.com/feelingofcomputing)) is
    monthly on Zoom, last Wednesday, three 15-minute talks, recorded. Volunteer in the monthly
    #present-company Slack thread. Likely dates 28 Oct, 25 Nov, 30 Dec. A good rehearsal.
- **A session Greg hosts himself**, e.g. "Read one hard paper together, with and without AI": 10–20
  people, 60–90 minutes, at Newspeak House, a library or a department common room, listed on Luma
  and posted to the communities below. Everyone in the room is a reader, and Greg watches them use
  it.
- **ReproducibiliTea journal clubs** — [bitss.org/reproducibilitea](https://www.bitss.org/reproducibilitea/).
  Volunteer-run journal clubs for early-career researchers, with chapters at UCL, Oxford and
  Cambridge, meeting about monthly in term. Offer the local organisers a session on reading papers
  critically. 2026 schedules unverified.
- **PhD training and library academic-skills sessions**: UCL Doctoral Skills, Oxford's
  [AI Competency Centre](https://oerc.ox.ac.uk/ai-centre/ai-centre-events), Cambridge Researcher
  Development, doctoral training partnerships, subject librarians. Workshops on using AI
  responsibly for reading the literature are in demand. Pitch a 60–90 minute workshop now; the
  lead time is about a term, so January to March 2027.
- **Advait Sarkar**, Microsoft Research Cambridge and University of Cambridge —
  [Tools for Thought](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/tools-for-thought/). Runs
  the CHI Tools for Thought workshops (2026: protecting and augmenting cognition with generative AI)
  and gives "AI as a tool for thought" talks. His agenda closely matches Spideryarn's. Ask for a
  demo, a lab talk and introductions. High for the network; medium for readers directly.

### Medium fit

- **Cambridge Rainbow Group seminars** (HCI, Computer Lab) —
  [talks.cam list 5401](https://talks.cam.ac.uk/list/index/5401/). Irregular. Alan Blackwell is the
  natural contact.
- **Researcher to Reader 2027** — [r2rconf.com](https://r2rconf.com/2026/06/26/the-r2r-conference-call-for-proposals-2027-is-now-open/).
  Scholarly-communication conference, London, 23–24 Feb 2027. The main call closed on 31 Aug;
  lightning talks have been taken by email until late December in past years (unverified for 2027).
  Publishers and librarians more than readers, but a lot of reach.
- **LILAC 2027** — [lilacconference.com](https://www.lilacconference.com/). Information-literacy
  conference, London, 21–23 Apr 2027. Past calls closed mid-November; the 2027 deadline is
  unverified. Librarians who teach reading and research skills, so each one reaches many students.
- **ALDinHE 2027** — [aldinhe.ac.uk](https://aldinhe.ac.uk/aldcon/aldcon26-call-for-proposals/).
  Learning developers in higher education. The call usually opens in November and closes in early
  January; it has lightning talks and a resource showcase.
- **Mindstone AI meetups** in London, Oxford, Cambridge and Bristol: monthly, three talks an
  evening (London 20 Oct 2026, Oxford 28 Oct). Professionals using AI, some of them heavy readers.
- **UCL Interaction Centre seminars** (Yvonne Rogers) and the **UCL Knowledge Lab seminar series**
  ([link](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/departments-and-centres/centres/ucl-knowledge-lab/seminar-series),
  AI in education). Pitch as research: augmenting rather than replacing reading, and for the
  Knowledge Lab, Learn mode and the memory research behind it. 2026–27 programmes unverified.
- **ToolsForThought.Rocks** ([luma.com/tftrocks-oct](https://luma.com/tftrocks-oct), online, 20
  minutes plus questions) and the **London PKM meetup** at Newspeak House
  ([luma.com/glylff5a](https://luma.com/glylff5a)). Whether either still runs is unverified.
- **Escaping Flatland and ACX London reader meetups**: communities of serious readers with no talk
  slots. Go to talk to people, or invite them to a session Greg hosts.

### Low fit

AI Demo Days ([luma.com/8pi189ji](https://luma.com/8pi189ji)), AI Tinkerers London and AI Builders
London (builders, not readers; fine for the technical story); the Cambridge Festival 2027 (10–25
Mar, deadline 13 Nov 2026, needs a university partner); Bett UK 2027 (ExCeL, 20–22 Jan, mostly
schools); the Centre for Educational Neuroscience and Oxford Computer Science seminars (academic
talks only); UX Brighton and the Oxford Gen AI Summit (programmes already set).
