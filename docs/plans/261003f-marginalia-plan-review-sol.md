### P1

1. **The proposed activation path is not safe as written.** `useAutoRun` can technically consume any `AutoRunTarget`, and arming before `OwnerMarginFeed` mounts is supported. But Marginalia remains `MODE_TARGET: none`, so its `ModeBoundary` has no `relations` target to retire if rendering fails before the hook claims the token. Reopening later can then spend an old press—the exact bug activation ownership prevents ([activation.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/web/activation.ts:113), [ModeBoundary.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/web/reader/ModeBoundary.tsx:151)). It also leaves `modeGenerates("marginalia")` false, so Commands will not disclose that it generates. The already-open command path returns early too ([Reader.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/web/reader/Reader.tsx:3313)), making its behaviour dependent on where the raw arm is inserted. Simpler: keep a Marginalia-open press nonce in `Reader`, pass it to `OwnerMarginFeed`, and run once after its read settles; or make switch activation a first-class mechanism including boundary retirement and disclosure. Do not bolt raw `armActivation` onto the branch.

2. **Timeline placement can attach a date to the wrong passage.** The stage tries occurrences until one supplies the date, and records that passage in `dating.when.at` ([timeline.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/timeline.ts:592)). The plan instead chooses the earliest surviving occurrence ([plan](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/docs/plans/261003f-marginalia-relation-words-and-timeline-events.md:32)). If an event is mentioned undated first and later as “By 12 July…”, the margin will show “at or before 12 July” beside the undated quote. Place `dated` at `when.at.blockId`; for `words`, place it at an occurrence containing `dating.phrase`.

3. **Timeline voice and year handling are wrong.** `event.label` is model output stored directly, not sliced article text ([timeline.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/timeline.ts:650)); it needs the AI face, not the author’s. Also, the panel suppresses a single common year only because its head says that year ([TimelinePanel.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbayajv6-marginalia-relations-timeline/src/web/TimelinePanel.tsx:340)). Marginalia has no such head, so “12 May” silently loses “2026”. Always include years in marginalia, or add equivalent context.

4. **Visitors can receive stale claims.** Both artefacts are carried across revisions. Owners hide stale lists, but public DTOs carry no stale verdict. A refreshed article can therefore show a visitor an old `BUT` against a surviving block id. Timeline’s quote check also cannot detect a stale publication-derived year (`yearFilled`). Either omit relations from visitors in v1, or compute freshness before public projection and omit stale artefacts. Timeline needs the same treatment for publication-dependent dates.

5. **The relation output contract permits silent partial or false success.** Validation drops bad entries but never requires every eligible paragraph to have exactly one answer. A model returning three rows for 100 paragraphs becomes a successful sparse artefact. Conversely, the first paragraph has no predecessor but must receive one of ten relations. Define the exact predecessor sequence, exclude the first or add `none`, and reject/count missing and duplicate classifications rather than merely taking “first wins”.

6. **The fingerprint does not match the prompt.** The plan says the shared `articleWithIds` prefix but fingerprints only blocks and tree. FAQ actually includes the metadata head too. A title/URL change can therefore alter model input while `relations` remains current. Fingerprint every input byte, and include the shared `paperwork(...)` instruction required of whole-article prompts.

Start by drawing only genuine reversals (`but`, `contrast`) or establish a per-section/article cap; the stored ten-way classification still permits widening later without another call.

9. **The placement example contradicts the prose specification.** It draws `SO` beside the preceding paragraph rather than “That is why…”, inviting an off-by-one implementation. Also make relation words focusable/tap-openable; a passive tooltip leaves touch and keyboard readers unable to learn what ambiguous labels such as “why” and “vs” mean.
