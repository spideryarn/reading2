No P0 findings. The main problems are notification loss across failed deploys and the lack of durable delivery state.

1. **P1 — `origin/main` is not necessarily the commit currently serving.**  
   Evidence: the plan equates pre-push `origin/main` with the live commit ([plan:18](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:18>)), but a push can advance `main` and then fail to build or promote ([deploy.ts:1286](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/scripts/deploy.ts:1286>)). Example: A is live; B adds a shipped report but its deployment fails; C later succeeds. Diffing B→C omits the report even though users went directly from A→C. `currentlyServing()` already returns the promoted deployment, including `meta.githubCommitSha` ([deploy.ts:1257](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/scripts/deploy.ts:1257>), [deploy-checks.ts:364](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/scripts/deploy-checks.ts:364>)).  
   Change: diff the SHA actually serving before the deployment against the verified new SHA. Refuse if its commit cannot be established. Add the A-live/B-failed/C-success test.

2. **P1 — the design cannot uphold “sent once per report”; it can lose or duplicate notifications.**  
   Evidence: the plan relies only on a transient commit diff and Resend’s 24-hour key ([plan:60](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:60>), [plan:77](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:77>)). After promotion, a crash before sending—or after some recipients—leaves no durable evidence. A normal redeploy then sees no transition; a manual retry after 24 hours may duplicate mail. `sendEmail` explicitly reports network failures as ambiguous because Resend may already have accepted them ([email.ts:116](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/email.ts:116>), [email.ts:240](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/email.ts:240>)). Grouping also means an overlapping retry with a different report set gets a different key and can duplicate within 24 hours.  
   Change: use a minimal durable outbox keyed by `(owner_id, report_id)` and reconcile every shipped-but-unnotified row, not only the latest diff. It does not require the status page claimed in the rejected alternative, and the measured absence of earlier non-admin reports removes the backfill problem. The simpler v1 is one email per report with a stable per-report identity.

3. **P2 — the two mass-mail guards do not cover the actual failure shapes.**  
   Evidence: the cap counts recipients, while the idempotency key contains every report id ([plan:53](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:53>), [plan:73](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:73>)). One reader with 17 reports produces a 257-character key, exceeding Resend’s 256-character limit. Resend also confirms that keys expire after 24 hours. [Resend idempotency documentation](https://resend.com/changelog/idempotency-keys). The proposed rerun cannot recover a legitimate run over 20 recipients because it applies the same cap again. Finally, “parsed at least one entry” does not detect partial parser drift that silently misses only new-format entries.  
   Change: use a bounded hash for the key; cap total deliveries/reports as well as recipients; provide an explicit, reviewed cap override or resumable batches; validate every entry in the generated object, not merely that the parse count is non-zero.

4. **P2 — adding this check to `AFTER_THE_FACT_CHECKS` makes the deploy summary lie.**  
   Evidence: the plan adds the email step to that list ([plan:70](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:70>)), but `summarise()` describes every after-the-fact-only failure as “Log verification was inconclusive” ([deploy.ts:1830](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/scripts/deploy.ts:1830>)). An email failure would therefore produce a false diagnosis.  
   Change: generalise that summary to say the deployment is live and name the failed after-the-fact checks; retain the no-rollback conclusion. Add an email-only failure test.

5. **P2 — the diagnostics gate is correctly rejected, but the claimed consent evidence is not true.**  
   Evidence: `consented` specifically controls diagnostic collection ([schema.ts:4322](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/db/schema.ts:4322>), [schema.ts:4497](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/db/schema.ts:4497>)); it must not gate this email. But “every reporter has seen” the hover text is false: the card opens only after a 240 ms hover/focus delay ([Tooltip.tsx:64](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/web/Tooltip.tsx:64>), [Tooltip.tsx:263](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/web/Tooltip.tsx:263>)), while the dialog deliberately removed its reply sentence ([FeedbackDialog.tsx:1109](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/web/FeedbackDialog.tsx:1109>)). The current privacy prose says the address is collected but not that a shipment email follows ([PrivacyPage.tsx:533](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/web/PrivacyPage.tsx:533>)).  
   Change: do not describe this as consent. Document the lawful basis and reasonable-expectation analysis, publish the privacy clause, and preferably put “We may email you when we act on this” in the dialog itself. Keep the message purely administrative; ICO guidance treats neutral customer-service messages as non-marketing, while legitimate interests still requires a purpose, necessity and balancing assessment. [ICO service-message guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/identify-direct-marketing/), [ICO legitimate-interests test](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/what-is-the-legitimate-interests-basis/).

6. **P2 — the subject and “fixed” wording overclaim what shipped means.**  
   Evidence: “Your feedback is now live” can imply that the private report itself was published ([plan:103](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:103>)). For a problem, “We’ve fixed it” is stronger than the status model: split reports count as shipped when at least one completed part shipped, even if another was declined ([feedback-endings.ts:116](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/scripts/feedback-endings.ts:116>)); the project also explicitly permits narrower or tweaked implementations.  
   Change: use “A change based on your feedback is now live” and “We’ve shipped a change in response to it.” Avoid the general changelog link unless the relevant change is guaranteed to appear there. Exact London dates may also be a day different for overseas readers; omit them unless needed for disambiguation.

7. **P3 — the Auth query is technically reasonable, but the address policy contradicts the schema documentation.**  
   Evidence: the plan chooses the current Auth address ([plan:67](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/docs/plans/261002f-email-readers-when-their-feedback-ships.md:67>)), while `reporter_email` is documented as the address intentionally retained for later contact ([schema.ts:4295](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/db/schema.ts:4295>)). Reading `auth.users` through the existing verified, read-only migrator connection adds no exposed endpoint and is not itself a security-defence change. `email_confirmed_at` is correct; `confirmed_at` may mean phone confirmation ([admin-accounts.ts:225](</home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbmmhrnw-feedback-shipped-email/src/store/admin-accounts.ts:225>)). `deleted_at` should be excluded. `banned_until` is a product-policy exclusion, not email validation: retaining it is conservative, but the plan should say banned readers are intentionally skipped.  
   Change: keep the current confirmed address for safety, but update the schema/docs to distinguish “address at filing” from “address used for outbound mail,” and test confirmed, deleted and banned accounts against production-shaped Auth rows.

Verdict: **Revise before build—the live-SHA trigger and durable delivery identity are blockers; the Auth filtering and diagnostics-consent decision are otherwise sound.**
