F1 — high — The fleet preview can consume the authoritative transcript’s rate-limit allowance. The plan says the fleet needs no change (`docs/plans/261019m…md:82-83`), but it uses the same hook and transcriber (`tools/fleet/web/src/DictationControl.tsx:82-90`), while its server allows only ten paid calls per context per minute (`tools/fleet/routes-transcribe.ts:95-110,372-396`). Successful five-second previews consume all ten slots by 50 seconds, so Stop can receive 429 and lose the real transcript. Give preview calls a separate, bounded allowance that always reserves capacity for the final call, or disable fleet previews until its route is changed. Add a test covering a dictation longer than one minute.

F2 — high — “It never touches the real recording” is not established on WebKit. The authoritative tape already starts a replacement recorder before stopping the old one (`src/web/mic-recording.ts:706-716`); adding a continuously running preview means three concurrent encoders at a tape rotation, potentially four when the 120-second and five-second rotations coincide. Safari/iPad recorder rotation is already explicitly unmeasured (`docs/project/dictation.md:681-686`), yet these are Stage 2’s target browsers. Preview failure handling cannot protect the real recorder from shared encoder/resource failure. The recording specification also permits failure when the UA cannot continue and warns about encoder resource exhaustion ([W3C MediaStream Recording](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediastream-recording/)). Make a real iPhone/iPad concurrency spike a gate, proving the authoritative file remains complete across preview and tape rotations; otherwise use a PCM/AudioWorklet preview or omit WebKit preview.

F3 — medium — Stage 1’s diagnosis is mostly right, but Safari/Firefox do not always arm immediately today. After capture, the hook owns the track at `src/web/useDictation.ts:1700-1704`, but a non-honoured remembered device awaits `listInputs()` at `:1724` before the non-live path sets `audioAt`, `listening`, and arms at `:1776-1787`. Thus the plan’s blanket claim at `docs/plans/261019m…md:29` misses another first-audio gap. Move all track-dependent setup—`audioAt`, phase, tape, meter track/context, and the `ended` listener—to immediately after `s.track = outcome.track`, before any device-label await. Test the held-`listInputs` fallback and Stop during that await.

F4 — medium — The planned `onaudiostart` rule is not race-safe enough. Stop sets `transcribing` while the session remains current and unfinished (`src/web/useDictation.ts:1255-1290`), whereas `onaudiostart` currently guards only current/finished before setting `listening` (`:1441-1452`). “Do nothing if already listening” still lets a late event after Stop regress `transcribing` to `listening`. Once ownership establishes `listening`, make `onaudiostart` state-free, or at minimum reject `stopRequested` and `liveGaveUp`; add late-`onaudiostart` and restart tests.

F5 — medium — The existing transcribe seam cannot meet the plan’s observability claims because it carries no preview/final purpose (`src/web/transcriber.ts:78-83`). Product requests admit exactly `audio`, `format`, and `context` (`src/routes.ts:8205-8221`), and any preview 5xx still reaches Sentry (`src/routes.ts:9541-9555`) even if the hook suppresses `setError`. The claim that distinguishing preview spend needs a new stored field is also wrong: `ai_calls.purpose` already exists (`src/db/schema.ts:3943-3946`); only the typed job inventory currently restricts transcription to `"dictation"` (`src/ai-call.ts:322-330`). Add a typed `"preview" | "final"` purpose through both product and fleet wires, record preview as its own purpose, and decide explicitly how preview server failures avoid Sentry. Do not defer this distinction.

F6 — medium — Preview needs its own lifecycle controller. The session’s existing `upload` controller owns authoritative part requests (`src/web/useDictation.ts:531-545`), and those requests deliberately survive ordinary Stop, the cap, and another box taking the microphone (`docs/project/dictation.md:647-650`). Reusing or aborting it for preview can cancel already-uploading real parts. Specify a separate preview recorder, abort controller, sequence number, and disabled flag; cancel them from every `finish` path, device change, superseding press, unmount, track end, and tape failure. Guard every result by session identity and sequence even after abort, since a transcriber is not guaranteed to honour its signal.

F7 — medium — This is a new data flow, though not a new recipient. `DICTATION_PROMISE` remains literally true (`src/web/DictationStrip.tsx:169-170`), but the current privacy record says audio arrives and leaves in a request associated with transcription after capture (`docs/project/privacy.md:351-357`), while Stage 2 sends repeated partial recordings before Stop and sends most speech twice. Revise the plan to describe this as a new transmission schedule and multiplicity, and include updates to `privacy.md` and `dictation.md`; the button promise may remain if the reviewed wording is still considered sufficient.

F8 — low — The duration floors are fine, but the cost estimate is not. Five seconds comfortably exceeds `MIN_MS = 600` (`src/web/mic-recording.ts:293-312`) and the server’s 2,000-character base64 floor (`src/transcribe.ts:113,220-221`). However, the plan’s `$0.0012/minute` conflicts with both the repository’s stated `$0.0045/minute` price (`src/ai-call.ts:3341-3355`) and the current [OpenRouter price](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-transcribe). Preview alone is approximately $0.0045 per recorded minute, making preview plus final about $0.009/minute before skipped segments or overlap. Correct the arithmetic and measure a twelve-segment run because this endpoint does not report usable per-call cost.

Simpler option: there is no clearly simpler cross-browser words preview. Reusing `dataavailable` chunks is unsafe because they are not necessarily standalone files; Web Speech does not cover Safari/Firefox; PCM/WAV capture is more code. The simplest safe sequence is to land Stage 1 first, then gate the second-recorder design on the WebKit spike and purpose-aware request plumbing.

Verdict: Stage 1 is sound after fixing the ownership-point and late-event details; Stage 2 is not ready to build until WebKit isolation, fleet limiting, request purpose, lifecycle, privacy, and cost are resolved.
