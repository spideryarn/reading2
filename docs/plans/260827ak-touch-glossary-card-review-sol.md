## Findings

1. **Must-fix — selection and long-press are misclassified as taps.** The plan decides solely from start/end distance ([plan:82](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/docs/plans/260827ak-touch-glossary-card.md:82)). A long-press can select a word without moving 10px; `pointerup` would then open the card and arm the `mouseup` swallower before `TableView` reads the selection ([TableView.tsx:512](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/TableView.tsx:512)). Require no non-collapsed selection, no intervening `contextmenu`, one primary pointer, and maximum movement—not merely endpoint distance. A second finger must cancel the gesture so pinch zoom cannot open or commit a term.

2. **Must-fix — the proposed test can pass with the event wiring broken.** On a clean tap the sequence is broadly `pointerover`/`pointerenter` → `pointerdown` → `pointerup` → compatibility `mouseup` → `click`. Touch gets implicit pointer capture, so `pointerup` normally bubbles from the original mark to `document`; `pointerover` also reaches the existing listener but is discarded by its touch gate ([useHoverCard.ts:286](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/useHoverCard.ts:286)). However, React’s root sees bubbling `mouseup` before a document bubble listener. Capture phase is load-bearing. The planned test only checks that `click.defaultPrevented` becomes true ([plan:118](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/docs/plans/260827ak-touch-glossary-card.md:118)); that passes even if `TableView.onMouseUp` already opened a chat. Mount a React ancestor with `onMouseUp` and `onClick`, use a `mark.term.chat` inside an `<a>`, dispatch both compatibility events, and prove neither callback runs. Also test unmount/remount listener cleanup. [Pointer Events specifies bubbling, implicit capture, and pre-down boundary events](https://www.w3.org/TR/pointerevents/).

3. **Must-fix — scrolling with a touch-open card leaves it covering the article.** `autoUpdate` deliberately follows the anchor during scroll ([useHoverCard.ts:176](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/useHoverCard.ts:176)). The plan’s `pointercancel` only drops the gesture ([plan:87](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/docs/plans/260827ak-touch-glossary-card.md:87)); it does not close the already-open card. A normal scroll can therefore carry the anchor off-screen while `shift` keeps the card at an edge. Track whether the card was touch-opened and close it on scroll, cancellation, resize/orientation, without changing desktop hover behavior.

4. **Must-fix — “first entry” is arbitrary and contradicts the card’s existing contract.** The card explicitly says that when two terms overlap, choosing one would be choosing for the reader, and renders a separate button for each ([ProseHoverCard.tsx:170](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/ProseHoverCard.tsx:170)). The plan nevertheless commits the first id ([plan:115](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/docs/plans/260827ak-touch-glossary-card.md:115)). The ordering carries no “best match” meaning. For multiple entries, a second tap should leave the card open and require one of the named **in the glossary** buttons.

5. **Should-fix — share the compatibility-event eater.** A term tap and a gist swipe cannot normally arm each other: their down-target surfaces are disjoint, and neither eater arms from mouse events. The real collision is stale state when Safari emits no compatibility click, followed quickly by a card-button tap at nearly the same place. `swipe.ts` clears its arm on every new pointerdown ([swipe.ts:231](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/swipe.ts:231)); the new mechanism must do exactly that. A shared window-capture eater would also prevent the unexplained drift between the established 120ms ([swipe.ts:120](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/swipe.ts:120)) and the plan’s 400ms. Keep 24px unless device evidence disproves it; measure the compatibility-event delay on an initial-scale and pinch-zoomed iPad.

6. **Should-fix — do not rely exclusively on `pointercancel`.** A conforming UA suppresses the pointer stream with `pointercancel` when it claims a permitted pan. The prose’s explicit `pan-y pinch-zoom` is favorable here ([styles.css:7530](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/styles.css:7530)). But selection, a disallowed-axis drag, an edge gesture, or a browser defect may still end in `pointerup`; implicit capture can leave its target as the original mark even when the finger is elsewhere. Track maximum movement through `pointermove`. This is not theoretical: [WebKit still records a case where `touch-action: manipulation` scrolls without `pointercancel`](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=240917).

7. **Should-fix — prefer card-only commit for prose.** The spine analogy is weak: spine bands are tiny and unreadable, while the prose already identifies the term and the card already offers a clear action. “Tap once to reveal; use the card button to commit” avoids accidental double-tap activation and resolves overlapping terms. If second-tap commit remains, limit it to exactly one term and add an explicit touch-open visual state. Do not reuse `data-term-open`, which means selected in Glossary mode ([styles.css:1384](/Users/greg/Dropbox/dev/experim/spideryarn2/src/web/styles.css:1384)).

8. **Note — double-tap zoom is already addressed more directly than the plan says.** `width=device-width` removes Safari’s old initial-scale click delay, but is not the durable gesture rule; [WebKit documents that distinction](https://webkit.org/blog/5610/more-responsive-tapping-on-ios/). The ancestor `td.text { touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom }` preserves pinch zoom while restricting other direct manipulation. Do not put `touch-action: manipulation` on `mark.term`: it is an inline element, where `touch-action` does not apply, and putting it on the cell would restore horizontal panning. Keep the real-iPad double-tap check.

9. **Should-fix — accessibility and visible state need decisions.** VoiceOver usually intercepts the physical touch; an inert `<mark>` has no accessible activation, and opening a `role="dialog"` without focus or announcement gives no usable preview. The plan must either provide an assistive activation path or explicitly document Glossary mode as the alternative. A transient open-anchor attribute is also needed; sticky iOS `:hover` is not a reliable state indicator. The device checklist should cover VoiceOver, callout selection, rotation, scroll dismissal, and anchor replacement.

**Verdict: not ready to build.**
