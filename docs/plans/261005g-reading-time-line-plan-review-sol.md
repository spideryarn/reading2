The conclusion stands as the most likely explanation, but the plan states it more certainly than the evidence permits.

1. **Medium — “there is no delay in the code” is too broad.** An enabled mount can postpone its opening GET while waiting for an earlier same-article write; that wait has no timeout ([useReadingTime.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useReadingTime.ts:114), [useReadingTime.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useReadingTime.ts:365)). After a GET answers, no chart is drawn unless stored or newly accumulated time reaches 35% of a block’s expected reading time ([reading-time.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/reading-time.ts:72), [reading-time.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/reading-time.ts:132)); unmatched or folded block IDs also produce no run ([spine-marks.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/spine-marks.ts:294)). A later one-second tick—not a flush—can then make the line appear ([useReadingTime.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useReadingTime.ts:277)). Conversely, when the response contains above-threshold totals for visible current blocks, it recomputes immediately and the Spine memo depends directly on the changed map; no re-measure or flush is needed ([useReadingTime.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useReadingTime.ts:372), [Spine.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/Spine.tsx:946)). The missing mount-time GET rules out the threshold explanation for this incident, but the prior-write wait remains a theoretical alternative. The plan should say “no timer or drawing delay matching this trace,” not “no delay in the code.”

2. **Medium — on-then-off is strongly supported, not proved.** A profile or auto-modes PATCH cannot causally turn reading time on: those clients read only their own response fields ([useProfile.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useProfile.ts:43), [auto-modes-setting.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/auto-modes-setting.ts:61)). Only the experimental setter optimistically changes `on`, which reruns the reading-time effect ([experimental-store.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/experimental-store.ts:516), [useReadingTime.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/useReadingTime.ts:402)). Therefore the 25 ms coupling is excellent evidence for `{experimental:true}`. But the route permits three different PATCH fields, and every response merely reports the current values ([routes.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/routes.ts:6761)). The final null row proves only that Experimental was off after the second write; it does not by itself identify either body. “Off-then-off” remains logically consistent only if the reading-time GET was an independent coincidence, such as the delayed prior-write path above. That is poorly supported by the trace, but not excluded.

   A client/server disagreement is also possible in general: the client starts at off/unknown, stays there on a failed read, and ignores repeated auth events for the same user ([experimental-store.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/experimental-store.ts:285), [experimental-store.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/experimental-store.ts:392), [experimental-store.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/experimental-store.ts:426)). Thus another-tab change can leave a mounted client stale. It does not fit this trace well: a live 200 arrived, valid responses map non-null directly to `on`, and an unknown/failed setting cannot be toggled through the normal controls. The plan’s “inferred step” heading is candid, but its bold statements at lines 47–53 should remain explicitly probabilistic rather than reading as proof ([plan](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/docs/plans/261005g-reading-time-line-waits-on-the-experimental-switch-not-on-a-timer.md:42)).

3. **Medium — the product choices omit material alternatives and consequences.** A fourth viable choice is: always fetch and display existing totals, but continue recording only while Experimental is on. That directly addresses “show the line immediately” without collecting new reading data from everyone, at the cost of permanent holes. A separate reading-time opt-in/opt-out is another material privacy choice. Options A and B should say that there is currently no dedicated way to disable or erase reading time; it persists with the article, appears in exports, and otherwise requires deleting the article or asking by email ([reading-time.md](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/docs/project/reading-time.md:95), [reading-time.md](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/docs/project/reading-time.md:138), [privacy.md](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/docs/project/privacy.md:405)). Option B is also ambiguous about Quiz: once reading-time state is available, Reader hands it to *Only what I’ve read* ([Reader.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/reader/Reader.tsx:1937)). “Readers with it off see nothing new” therefore requires explicitly gating Quiz too. Finally, the scope is every owner, not merely every paying reader.

4. **Low — the caller inventory is wrong.** The plan says Dock and Settings are the two callers of `set`, but the command bar is a third direct caller ([CommandBar.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/CommandBar.tsx:927), alongside [Dock.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/Dock.tsx:4025) and [SettingsSection.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbdxufdw-reading-time-line-late/src/web/SettingsSection.tsx:84)). All three still require an explicit reader action, so this does not weaken the diagnosis.

Plainly: the switch press is by far the best explanation, and no non-experimental PATCH has a causal path to start reading time. The plan should present that as a strong inference, narrow its “no delay” claim, and expand the product/privacy choices.
