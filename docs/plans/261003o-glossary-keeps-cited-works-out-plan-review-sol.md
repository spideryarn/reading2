1. **P1 — The `et al` guard contradicts the proposed semantic rule.** [plan:59](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/docs/plans/261003o-glossary-keeps-cited-works-out-citations-are-not-terms.md:59), [glossary.ts:301](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/src/glossary.ts:301)  
   `/\bet\s+al\b/i` can remove a legitimate term: an article about the abbreviation “et al.”, a work or organisation titled “Et Al.”, or a study discussed substantively but referred to throughout as “Saha et al.” The last case directly violates “discussed stays.” `toEntries` is mechanically the correct seam for fresh model output, and no other model-output path bypasses it: `generateGlossary` always calls `buildGlossary`, which calls `toEntries` ([glossary.ts:1670](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/src/glossary.ts:1670)). Existing appended entries do bypass it through `previous` ([glossary.ts:833](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/src/glossary.ts:833)), while reader-added terms bypass it intentionally ([glossary-added.ts:68](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/src/glossary-added.ts:68)). The version bump prevents old entries being appended, and `inheritIds` transfers identity rather than content, so those are sound. I would drop the regex guard and ship the measured prompt rule alone. If a hard invariant is later needed, it must use citation context or verified citation mentions, not the spelling `et al`.

2. **P1 — “Cited versus discussed” is the right product line, but the planned wording is not operational enough.** [plan:48](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/docs/plans/261003o-glossary-keeps-cited-works-out-citations-are-not-terms.md:48), [plan:121](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fbzn97q5-glossary-without-citations/docs/plans/261003o-glossary-keeps-cited-works-out-citations-are-not-terms.md:121)  
   A model can apply the distinction, but it needs contrasting cases. “Saha et al. found X” is normally just attribution and should be out even though the article says what they found. Conversely, a literature review may analyse a paper’s method and findings while naming it only by author-year; that work is genuinely discussed. Other likely misses are a titled paper or linked author used only as a source, which has no year or `et al`, and a person who is cited in one passage but discussed historically elsewhere, who should remain while citation-shaped aliases go. I would define the test as: *if the name’s only job is to attribute or support a claim, it belongs to Citations; keep it only when understanding who/what it is matters independently of locating the source.* Add worked BAD/GOOD pairs covering attribution verbs, a genuinely analysed work, and a person with both roles.
