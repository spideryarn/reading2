# GPT Sol's review of plan 261101c

Read-only, `gpt-5.6-sol`, effort high, 2026-11-01. The plan's § GPT Sol's plan review says what was taken. Up: [261101c](261101c-citations-list-on-haiku-and-better-notes.md).

1. **The Haiku artefact will never be current in the pipeline or Metadata.** The plan says freshness is unaffected (`docs/plans/261101c…:24-29`), but both expected stamps still use `CAPABLE_MODEL`: `src/pipeline.ts:6267-6274` and `src/store/pg.ts:3792-3805`. `sameStamp` treats Haiku as a different generation (`src/store/artifacts.ts:1195-1212`). Fix both expectations to `modelFor("bibliography", power)`—standard power in the store—and test pipeline/store agreement at both powers.

2. **Stage 1 can ship a 61%-recall run while claiming Haiku “finds them all.”** “Inside H2’s spread” (`plan:60-64`) includes Synesthesia’s 61–99% spread (`investigation:103-106`) and drops the prior review’s false-row-rate requirement (`261101a plan:147-149`); the truth is still incomplete enough that a clean false-row rate cannot currently be computed (`investigation:109-111`). Fix the truth first, then require every repetition to meet an absolute per-article floor, count failures as zero, preserve the short-article baseline, and bound false-row rate. Otherwise explicitly ask Greg to accept the observed 61% worst case.

3. **The new “first 300” rule contradicts the implementation.** The plan says `citations/8` is cut by order (`plan:39-41`), but `keepLeanedOnMost` still sorts by relevance (`src/bibliography.ts:753-770`). Fix the cap to retain the first 300 after folding, or rename/restate the intended relevance rule; test an over-300 answer whose relevance order differs from citation order.

4. **A versioned capped note cannot work for public readers.** `BibliographyPanel` renders owner and visitor lists through the same `cappedNote`, but the public DTO deliberately drops `version` (`src/public-types.ts:791-803`, `src/public/dto.ts:796-805`). Passing `bibliography.version` as proposed will either not compile for visitors or show old public lists with the new 300/order wording. Simpler fix: store and expose a safe cap descriptor such as `{ limit, selection }`, with a `citations/7` fallback, instead of teaching UI copy to interpret prompt versions.

5. **`citation-blurbs` is not bounded by the registry’s 80-row cap.** The plan conflates the two (`plan:43-45`). Registry attachment stops at 80 (`src/citation-registry.ts:227-259`), but blurbs receives every bibliography row (`src/citation-blurbs.ts:743-785`); evidence processes every identifier and only separately caps title searches at 60 (`src/citation-evidence.ts:999-1034`). A list can also contain 300 model rows plus up to 200 code rows. The downstream pass must assert `counts.rows === bibliography.citations.length`, every row has a terminal entry, `partial === false`, and no transient rows—not merely that the step returned before its deadline. Test the worst allowed list, not only Butlin’s near-complete 250.

6. **The Stage 2 sample can silently hide an arm’s missing or malformed notes.** The proposed “first eight shared works” preserves the current script’s behaviour: missing rows are skipped and later rows substituted (`evals/citations-recall/why-pairs.ts:53-59`). Each arm can therefore be judged on a different, easier subset. Freeze a manifest of truth-work keys before running arms; score exactly those keys for every arm, and count an absent row/`why` as a loss or error. Keep prompt-development examples out of the held-out ship set—the first 24 pairs already informed N1.

7. **The Stage 2 decision rule does not establish parity.** Raw wins on 48 correlated pairs, judged once by Opus (`plan:78-83,103-106`), can call a noisy tie “level”; Opus may also have a family/style preference toward Sonnet. Predeclare a non-inferiority margin and confidence rule clustered by article, use at least one independent cross-family blind judge plus adjudication, and put “relevance clearly off” into the gate rather than merely tallying it. Influence cannot be judged from citing passages alone; either omit it from this yardstick, expect conservative `null`, or provide separate evidence.

8. **N2 lacks a safe join, freshness, and ledger design.** `citedAt` is block IDs, not passages, and direct mentions are capped (`src/types.ts:5621-5637`). The plan does not say what happens when a batch omits an ID, duplicates one, invents one, or fails; “replace” can silently erase valid first-pass notes. Require exact ID-set validation, resolve passages from blocks plus bibliography-only references, preserve first-pass notes or fail atomically, and prove citation rows/IDs are byte-for-byte unchanged. If N1 ships, bump beyond `citations/8`; if N2 ships, include its notes-prompt version/model in the composite stamp and give the second pass a distinct ledger job so its cost is measurable.

9. **“Production path” parity should not be implemented by copying the request.** The current eval manually reconstructs production’s body (`evals/citations-recall/run.ts:124-187`), while the plan proposes another arm that merely claims to send exactly the same fields. Extract one `bibliographyRequest` builder used by both `generateBibliography` and the eval, then parity-test the complete wire body. Also update the scheduler budget and honest UI timing: the scheduler has a bibliography-specific budget (`src/jobs.ts:1076-1097`), while the UI still promises under one minute / two or three minutes (`src/web/BibliographyPanel.tsx:1148-1153`) against measured one-to-two / two-to-four minutes.

No separate checkpoint, export, MCP, or pricing-table migration appears necessary: Bibliography has no paid-answer checkpoint, exports serialize the artefact, MCP exposes no uncapped bibliography list, and the spend ledger records the actual wire model. The public DTO issue above is the material reader boundary.
