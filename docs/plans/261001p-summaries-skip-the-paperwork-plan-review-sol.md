No P0 findings. The diagnosis is broadly right, but I would change the plan in several places before building it.

1. **P1 — The paperwork definition can suppress substantive content.** Confidence: high. The carve-out only protects an article *about* funding or conflicts, but those facts can matter inside an article about something else: a sponsor’s role may qualify the evidence, ethics approval may constrain the method, and data availability may affect reproducibility. “It tells a reader nothing” and “do not count it among caveats or limits” are too categorical ([plan](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-takeaway/docs/plans/261001p-summaries-skip-the-paperwork-and-lead-with-the-takeaway.md:59)). A normal limitations section is not on the list and should survive, but the current wording leaves nearby boundary cases unsafe.

   I would define paperwork by its role, not merely its label: “Treat these as paperwork only when they merely record publication administration. If the piece uses one as evidence, reasoning, method, limitation, disclosed standpoint, or part of its conclusion, it is content and the normal rules apply.” Then say “normally omit” rather than “never summarise.”

2. **P1 — The proposed Structure rule contradicts three existing requirements and leaves depth-1 questions unresolved.** Confidence: high. Its examples are expressly topic labels, while `SYSTEM` requires a claim or move; they can fall below the deeper 22-word floor; and a paperwork node at depth 1 is still commanded to have exactly one substantive question ([hierarchy.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-takeaway/src/hierarchy.ts:119)). The plan explicitly exempts only the word floor ([plan](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-takeaway/docs/plans/261001p-summaries-skip-the-paperwork-and-lead-with-the-takeaway.md:75)).

   I would make the exception complete and local: “For a paperwork-only range, keep the node for coverage. Its gist is a short neutral label; this is the sole exception to the claim-or-move rule and the length floor. Omit `question`, even at depth 1. When writing ancestor and root gists, ignore that child.” Absence of `question` is already a supported stored state.

   I would also not defer the deepening cascade. `EXPAND_SYSTEM` independently requires a 22–32-word claim gist and may require a question ([hierarchy-expand.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/hierarchy-expand.ts:286)). “Rarely long enough” is not a contract. Either give it the same exception and bump `expand/6` to `expand/7`, or mechanically prevent a known paperwork-only node from being deepened. The prompt rule is the simpler option here.

3. **P1 — Keeping `simple/2` is right; having no separate Simple prompt stamp is not.** Confidence: high. `simple/2` is genuinely a shape version and `isUsableSimpleSummary` rejects anything else ([types.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/types.ts:4652), [types.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/types.ts:4865)), so it should not be bumped for prose changes. But `SIMPLE_VERSION` currently claims to be bumped whenever the prompt changes and is also used for freshness ([simple-summary.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/simple-summary.ts:120)). Leaving it unchanged makes `outdated` permanently false.

   The plan’s “until the reader presses Write it again” is also inaccurate: a settled current summary only shows that button after staleness, a changed profile, an active job, or failure—not merely because the reader wants a new rendition ([SimplePanel.tsx](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/web/SimplePanel.tsx:69)).

   I would split this now: retain `SIMPLE_ARTIFACT_VERSION = "simple/2"`, add a prompt version stored separately on new artefacts, allow its absence on legacy `simple/2` rows, and treat absent/older prompt versions as usable-but-outdated. If that expansion is deliberately out of scope, the plan should plainly say existing summaries will not receive this change without an administrative forced rerun.

4. **P1 — The measurement does not test the rule’s most dangerous boundary and is too small to distinguish the takeaway effect from sampling noise.** Confidence: high. All three articles appear to contain unambiguously administrative paperwork ([plan](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/docs/plans/261001p-summaries-skip-the-paperwork-and-lead-with-the-takeaway.md:144)). None tests substantive funding, industry ties, ethics, data access, or author standpoint. The blind judge also excludes Structure and the Simple/Fuller levels even though their prompts change.

   Three articles with one draw per arm give only three before/after observations per mode and three control observations; that can catch a glaring regression, but not establish that conclusion quality moved beyond ordinary variation. I would:

   - Replace at least one paper with a boundary case where funding, disclosure, or ethics is substantive.
   - Blind-judge Structure’s paperwork nodes and depth-1 questions too.
   - Judge all changed summary levels, or change only Brief.
   - Prefer two draws per arm on a smaller, sharper corpus if the budget is fixed.
   - Use explicit pass criteria: no administrative-only material outside its neutral Structure node; no missing substantive disclosure; no invented implication; no question on paperwork-only nodes.

5. **P1 — `tweets/6` does not make an existing thread “read as outdated.”** Confidence: high. `ThreadResponse` has no `outdated` field ([types.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/types.ts:407)), and `loadTweets` returns only `stale` ([pg.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/store/pg.ts:3325)). Worse, Metadata’s Tweets currency check compares only the source fingerprint, despite `PROMPT_VERSION` now being exported ([pg.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/store/pg.ts:2964)). The panel has no standing rewrite button and relies on Metadata for settled current threads.

   The bump itself is correct, and `sentIds(version) >= 5` remains correct ([tweets.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/tweets.ts:201)). I would additionally fix Metadata’s Tweets arm to compare version and model through `sameStamp`, with a regression test. Adding an API `outdated` field is optional if the product intentionally keeps it silent, but the plan must stop claiming that such a read state already exists.

6. **P2 — `100 → 80` is a plausible request, but the evidence does not support it as a reliable limit.** Confidence: high. The observed 186 words are 86% above the 100-word target and 24% above the 150-word stated ceiling—not “a third over its own ceiling.” The earlier one-third drift does not explain this run. Because generation validation still accepts 240 words ([types.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/types.ts:4670)), the model can ignore the new 130-word prompt ceiling exactly as it ignored 150.

   Keeping the stored 240-word read boundary is correct. I would either add a separate generation-only cap of 130, leaving legacy reads at 240, or make shipping conditional on the measured runs showing that the two-paragraph wording reliably works. The two-paragraph change is probably the stronger lever. Nothing else reads `PITCH.brief.words`; `PITCH` is private and consumed only by `simpleSystem`.

7. **P2 — “What it means for a reader” still invites invented advice.** Confidence: high. It pulls against “why THE PIECE says it matters” and “Only what the piece says” ([simple-summary.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/simple-summary.ts:245)). Models can reconcile the conflict by treating an inferred implication as something the piece “means.”

   I would use: “End on the piece’s main conclusion and any implication it explicitly states.” For Tweets: “End on the piece’s conclusion or final landing. If it deliberately reaches no conclusion, end on the central question it leaves unresolved.” That preserves essays and negative-result papers without forcing advice or forcing every work into a conclusion shape. “Why it matters” can then clearly mean motivation; “takeaway” means the result or landing.

8. **P2 — The hierarchy bump misses promised collateral, and the claim about `evals/summaries` is false for the proposed placement.** Confidence: high. The repository has explicitly deferred the block-id prose rule until the next hierarchy bump ([article-prompt.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/article-prompt.ts:117), [prompting-guide.md](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/docs/project/prompting-guide.md:90)); `toc/10` is that bump, so the plan should include it and update those comments.

   Literal `toc/9` pins also exist in the hoist and expansion tests, as the plan partly notes. No additional runtime consumer of the literal appeared. However, `evals/summaries` slices only from `GISTS` to `QUESTIONS` and from `QUESTIONS` to `OUTPUT` ([production-prompt.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/evals/summaries/production-prompt.ts:80)). A `PAPERWORK` section interpolated beside `plainWords` near the end will not enter either slice, so that eval’s live arm does not move. Either integrate the relevant exceptions into those sections or remove that claim and rely on the new full-prompt harness.

9. **P2 — The causal diagnosis is mostly right, but the last-tweet diagnosis is not exclusive and the author fix can be simpler.** Confidence: medium-high. The nine-author post is strongly encouraged by the duplicated full byline plus “Refer to them by surname,” together with the requirement to attribute contested claims ([tweets.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/tweets.ts:236), [tweets.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/tweets.ts:310)). So the diagnosis is sound as a proximate cause, though the full byline remains in the article head too.

   The conflict-of-interest ending is probably jointly caused by three things: it is last in document/tree order, the prompt says to carry caveats, and THE LAST POST demands an unresolved matter. Changing only the last-post sentence is not a complete explanation; the paperwork rule is doing most of the repair.

   Since `Meta` already has structured `authors` as well as a free-text byline ([types.ts](/home/greg/code/spideryarn2/.claude/worktrees/fb8m-8f-summaries-lead-with-the-takeaway/src/types.ts:1447)), I would avoid asking the model to count and parse a raw byline where possible. For more than two structured authors, render a direct instruction such as: “This has multiple authors. Attribute collective claims to ‘the authors’; never list them.” Use “first author and colleagues” only if naming that person materially helps attribution.

Overall: the direction is good, the hierarchy and stamp details need correction, and the paperwork rule needs a role-based exception before it is safe to share across modes.
