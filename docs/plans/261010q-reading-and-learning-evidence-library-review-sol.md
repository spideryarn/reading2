PROCEED WITH CHANGES

1. The evidence-quality controls are too weak. “Check a sample of citations” is insufficient for twelve model-written reports. Require:

   - Every reference opened—not recalled or taken from a search snippet.
   - Metadata and DOI checked against the publisher, Crossref, or PubMed; never invent a DOI, and allow stable URLs for books or conference papers.
   - Every load-bearing claim manually checked against the source, with page/table/abstract location recorded.
   - A structured evidence row: design, N, population, text/task, comparator, outcome, delay, effect size/CI, limitations and directness to Spideryarn.
   - Search queries, databases, cutoff date, inclusion/exclusion rules, citation chaining, corrections/retractions and null results.
   
   “Meta-analysis = strong” is not a sound grading rule. Grade risk of bias, consistency, precision and publication bias separately from applicability. A strong L2-vocabulary result may still be weak evidence for expert adults reading research papers. Also change “primary sources” because meta-analyses are secondary sources.

2. The topic map has four material gaps:

   - **Search and navigation**: Search mode is absent. Cover within-document search, exploratory search, information foraging and “searching as learning.”
   - **Purpose and personalisation**: Reader Profile and purpose-based recommendations express a core product claim but have no topic. Start with Rouet, Britt & Durik’s 2017 RESOLV model: reading goals shape strategy and attention.
   - **Active generation versus receiving AI output**: This cuts across Summary, Structure, FAQ, Diagram, Chat and Explore. Chi & Wylie’s 2014 ICAP framework is an essential lead; generated artefacts may be less educational than asking readers to explain, question or draw themselves. [Chi & Wylie 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823)
   - **Motivation, curiosity and learner control** should not be folded into prequestions and tutor tone. Add Hidi & Renninger 2006, Kang et al. 2009 and Gruber et al. 2014.

3. Several counter-findings should be explicit leads:

   - McNamara, Kintsch, Songer & Kintsch 1996: higher coherence helped low-knowledge readers but could reduce deeper processing by knowledgeable readers—a direct challenge to uniformly simplified summaries. [McNamara et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1)
   - Morehead, Dunlosky & Rawson 2019 and Urry et al. 2021 did not support a confident longhand-over-laptop advantage. [Morehead et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09468-2), [Urry et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797620965541)
   - Nesbit & Adesope 2006 distinguishes constructing, modifying and merely viewing maps; Diagram/Sketch must not inherit benefits demonstrated for learner-generated maps. [Nesbit & Adesope 2006](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543076003413)
   - Gier, Kreiner & Natz-Gonzalez 2009 found inaccurate pre-highlighting harmed both comprehension and calibration—important for generated decoration and Quotes. [Gier et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19650523/)
   - Ginns 2006 on spatial contiguity/split attention belongs with Marginalia, the band and diagrams.
   - The Sparrow “Google effect” needs Camerer et al. 2018 and Hesselmann 2020 prominently labelled as failed/mixed replications, not merely “and its replication.”
   - Kosmyna et al. 2025 remains a contested preprint; “cognitive surrender” is framing, not an empirical finding. Keep both out of the evidential backbone.

4. Topics 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 12 are too broad. Most problematic:

   - Split retrieval/feedback from prequestions, question generation and metacomprehension.
   - Split human scaffolding/desirable difficulty from evaluations of AI tutors.
   - Split cognitive offloading from AI trust, hallucination and verification.
   - Split gloss/vocabulary learning from hypertext navigation and disorientation.
   - Split skimming/multiple-pass strategies from headings, structure and signalling.
   - Split diagrams/multimedia from argument maps, timelines and learner-generated drawing.
   - Make topic 12 a bounded prior-art/evaluation-method census; otherwise it duplicates almost every topical researcher.

5. The digest’s mode-by-mode presentation is useful, but its unit of judgment should be the **product claim**, not the whole mode. Build a claim–evidence matrix first, separating direction, certainty, directness, population, immediate comprehension, delayed retention, transfer, calibration, time and perceived ease. Then derive mode sections. “Untested,” “indirect evidence,” “no reliable effect” and “evidence of harm” must remain distinct; “well-founded” otherwise overstates analogies from classrooms or L2 learners.

   - The evergreen `docs/project` overview must remain a thin hub; `digest.md` owns the conclusions, avoiding two homes for the evidence.
   - Add it under Vision’s `↳` block in `CLAUDE.md`, not as an eighth entry point; link down from `vision.md` and back up from the new doc.
   - Add the tutorial to `docs/project/tutorials.md`. Also include existing `261005c-what-makes-a-longer-summary…` and `261009b-what-a-peer-reviewer-needs…` in the prior-work map so researchers do not repeat them.
