The diagnosis is correct, but the proposed singleton ref is not robust enough to approve.

- **F1 — P1 — A singleton pointer type can violate reveal-before-commit.** The plan stores only the latest type, clears it on `pointercancel`, and consumes it at the next click (`docs/plans/260924c-ipad-first-tap-on-the-rail-shows-the-card.md:53-76`). This loses both press ordering and whether the card was open when that press began. The existing implementation explicitly documents why a singleton is unsafe when compatibility clicks are delayed/grouped (`src/web/useHoverCard.ts:489-496`) and tests two presses arriving before their clicks (`tests/link-tap-escapes.test.tsx:320-332`). It also requires a click after `pointercancel` to reveal but never commit (`tests/link-tap-escapes.test.tsx:515-526`). Under this plan, that click is read as mouse and `bandPress` jumps (`src/web/Spine.tsx:310-315`). A fast A-then-B tap whose clicks arrive together similarly makes B jump without B’s card having been shown. **Suggested change:** retain an ordered, short-lived press record containing type and `armedId` at pointerdown. A click may jump only when its own uncancelled press began with that same band armed; missing, canceled, stale, or ambiguous records may reveal but never jump. Add grouped A/B, fast double-tap-before-first-click, and post-cancel-click tests.

- **F2 — P2 — Rail-level pointerdown does not cover touch adjustment from outside the rail.** The plan only records events bubbling through `<aside>` (`plan:53-55`). The repository already demonstrates the relevant WebKit sequence: pointer events land beside a target while the adjusted click lands on it (`tests/link-tap-escapes.test.tsx:231-240`; `src/web/useHoverCard.ts:662-668`). If a finger lands immediately beside the 12px rail and WebKit adjusts the click onto a band, `<aside>` sees no pointerdown; the mislabelled mouse click still jumps. This exact rail manifestation needs an iPad to confirm, so I am not treating it as P1, but the plan’s “same gesture” claim is incomplete. **Suggested change:** collect press provenance above the rail—preferably document capture with ordered consumption—or explicitly accept the limitation. Add a test whose pointerdown target is outside `<aside>` and whose click target is a band.

- **F3 — P2 — `useHoverCard` is behaviorally correct, but the plan overstates its existing evidence.** The mislabel branch correctly substitutes a fresh touch/pen press and drops the bad pointer id (`src/web/useHoverCard.ts:786-824`). A term inside a link then follows the same click path (`src/web/useHoverCard.ts:827-854`), and `ProseHoverCard` gives the inner term precedence (`src/web/ProseHoverCard.tsx:278-284`, `459-469`). A bare term is correctly decided at pointerup. However, the WebKit-mislabel tests exercise a plain external link (`tests/link-tap-escapes.test.tsx:258-265`, `304-317`); the term-inside-link test uses a correctly labelled touch click (`tests/link-tap-escapes.test.tsx:407-417`). Also, “never consults the click’s type” is literally inaccurate for bare terms: `clicked` calls `clickPress` before `swallowed` (`src/web/useHoverCard.ts:827-830`), although the outcome is type-independent. **Suggested change:** add first/second mislabelled-click coverage for a glossary term inside a link and reword the bare-term claim to “does not base its action on the click’s type.”

The root diagnosis is directly supported by the reopened [WebKit bug 282988](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=282988): touch pointerdown/up report `touch`, while click reports `mouse`. BlockGutter is harmless as claimed: both non-empty `mouse` and `touch` values take the same copy branch (`src/web/BlockGutter.tsx:346-358`). Keyboard, ordinary mouse, pen, React bubbling, and Floating UI’s composed handlers otherwise look sound.

**Verdict: Refuse as written — the diagnosis is right, but F1 leaves an established P1 reveal-before-commit violation.**
