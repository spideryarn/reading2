1. **High — the placement test still admits body sections when heading levels are inverted or inconsistent.** In the proposed `summaryAt` placement check ([src/matter.ts:1150](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:1150)), only earlier headings at the candidate’s level or higher count. Input: title `h1`; `Introduction` `h2`; 500 words; `Key points` `h1`, still inside `matterWindows`; 400 words; then another `h1`. The earlier `h2` does not outrank the `h1`, so `Key points` passes and its body section folds. This is plausible after PDF extraction and directly violates Greg’s warning. The simpler rule—no earlier non-label, non-title heading at all—finds all three corpus cases: arXiv has none, NIST’s preceding headings are known labels, and PMC’s label is block 1.

2. **High — headingless prose before a paragraph label is invisible to the placement test.** `matterWindows` is a model-input window, not a semantic front-matter boundary: it counts at most 200 characters per block and allows up to 40%/120 blocks ([src/matter.ts:93](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:93), `matterWindows`). Input: a newsletter or blog with ten unheaded opening paragraphs, then a text block `2. Highlights`, followed by 500 words and later headings. `normaliseLabel` removes the section number ([src/matter-summary.ts:23](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter-summary.ts:23)); there is no preceding heading to veto it; the 500 words fold as a front summary. The placement rule needs evidence that substantive prose has not already begun, not merely that no heading recorded it.

3. **High — the heading extent can absorb real body sections when extraction gives the summary a coarser level.** `sectionRanges` ends only at a heading of the same or higher rank ([src/section-ranges.ts:37](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/section-ranges.ts:37)). Input: `Executive Summary` `h1`, 300 summary words, `Introduction` `h2`, 500 body words, `Methods` `h2`, 300 body words, then `Part II` `h1`; the candidate is 1,100 words, under the cap and potentially under the half-article floor, so all three sections fold. The same happens when `Introduction` was extracted as ordinary text: neither the heading-section boundary nor the paragraph rule’s numbered-title boundary sees it. Whole-or-nothing prevents a half-summary, but does not distinguish a whole summary from summary-plus-prose.

4. **Medium — the extent sentinels reject common summary contents.** The proposed numbered-title stop and “block opening Abstract or Keywords” rule are too lexical ([src/matter.ts:1106](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:1106), `endsSummary`). Inputs:

   - `Key points` followed by `1. Reduce latency` and its explanation: the numbered key point is read as the next article section, leaving only the label; the one-short-block rule then drops the genuine summary.
   - `Significance` followed by “Subjects were adults aged…”: the existing `KEYWORDS_LABEL` accepts `Subjects` as a keyword-label prefix ([src/matter.ts:367](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:367)), so the real significance paragraph is excluded.
   - A paragraph-form `Executive Summary` followed by an internal `Findings` heading: the paragraph-label rule stops at the first heading, so the candidate again contains only its label and is dropped.

   The stop should require label syntax such as a whole label or `Keywords: …`, and a numbered line needs stronger section evidence than “short and capitalised.”

5. **Medium — classifier precedence can split a summary despite the model-only schema.** `combineMatter` assigns an overlapping segment the classifier’s kinds first (`model ?? label`) ([src/matter.ts:1427](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:1427)). Input: a `Key points` label followed by three short bullets linking to later headings. The label rule admits the whole box as `summary`; the model, despite its instruction, returns the three bullets as `contents`, which can pass contents evidence. The union becomes a tiny `summary` stretch containing only “Key points” plus a `contents` stretch containing its bullets. The own-stub join guard runs too late to restore the summary. Make an admitted summary candidate win overlaps, or discard classifier candidates intersecting one.

6. **Medium — adjacent front-summary kinds collapse to one misleading stub.** Touching equal-kind segments are joined before the own-stub rule ([src/matter.ts:1435](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:1435)). Input: an `Executive Summary` section immediately followed by `Key points`; both are stored as `summary`, so they rejoin. `stubLabel` then reads only the combined stretch’s first block ([src/web/matter-view.ts:223](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/web/matter-view.ts:223)) and displays “Executive summary”; the Key points label disappears from the folded presentation. Summary-to-summary joins must also preserve label identity, most simply by never rejoining separate summary candidates.

7. **Low — the planned schema-enum test does not prove that the request is byte-identical.** `matterInput` also depends on `shownHeadings`, whose priority flag comes from `isLabel`/`labelKinds` ([src/matter.ts:849](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:849), [src/matter.ts:928](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbhnw9xq-fold-front-summaries/src/matter.ts:928)). Keeping summary labels in the separate `isSummaryLabel` map makes the split sound; adding them to `labelKinds` or widening `isLabel` would reprioritise a middle `Highlights` heading, change the user message, and invalidate cached answers even though `KIND_SCHEMA` remains unchanged. The regression should compare the complete serialized `matterRequest` on a fixture with a summary heading between the windows, not only inspect its enum.
