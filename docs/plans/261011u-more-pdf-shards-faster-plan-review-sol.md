### Findings

- **F1 — P1: The one-wave premise is false.** Width 100 exceeds the chunk counts of the two cited documents, not every PDF the product accepts. A 250-page PDF may already produce up to 250 one-page chunks when word or byte bounds force it; the proposed third-size arm explicitly expects about 130 chunks. That arm changes both call size and wave count, so concurrency remains part of the result. Evidence: [plan:21](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:21), [plan:72](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:72), [pdf-read.ts:624](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:624), [pdf-read.ts:631](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:631).

- **F2 — P1: Stage 1 cannot obtain its promised decomposition from `ai_calls`.** The ledger has duration and token counts, but no page range or chunk key. Its `attempt` is the transport-retry number and is explicitly null for PDF calls because that path owns its retry loop. It therefore cannot distinguish initial transcription calls, quality re-asks, or page recovery. Add per-chunk instrumentation to the eval, or narrow Stage 1 to duration-versus-output-token analysis without claiming first-wave/re-ask attribution. Evidence: [plan:36](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:36), [schema.ts:3924](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/db/schema.ts:3924), [schema.ts:3931](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/db/schema.ts:3931), [pdf-read.ts:967](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:967).

- **F3 — P1: The experiment must time the internal phases separately.** All cuts are prepared serially before fan-out; ordinary quality re-asks are serial within their chunk; recovery reads every page serially; and phase-2 recovery occurs in the ordered fold after the whole fan-out. Front-matter and author calls add unrelated serial latency afterward. These are materially different critical paths: the existing evidence shows a 69-second wire wave becoming a 142–248-second extract. Record preparation, initial wave, quality re-asks, transport/backoff, phase-1 recovery, phase-2 recovery, and auxiliary passes separately. Evidence: [pdf-read.ts:2999](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:2999), [pdf-read.ts:3270](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:3270), [pdf-read.ts:3407](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:3407), [pdf-read.ts:3526](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:3526), [pdf-read.ts:3549](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:3549), [pdf-read.ts:264](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:264).

- **F4 — P1: Two runs per cell cannot safely drive the 25% production threshold without stronger controls.** The recorded width-100 runs vary from 142 to 248 seconds; the “median” of two observations is just their midpoint and cannot characterize this tail. Moreover, `sharedGate` persists across runs: an arm that meets a 429 can leave the next arm starting below width 100, even if that next run records no new 429. Use fresh processes or a fresh injected `WidthGate(100)`, balance/interleave arm order, retain failed runs, and confirm the winning arm with additional repetitions. Record gate start/end width, answered provider/model, and cache-read/write tokens. Evidence: [plan:50](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:50), [plan:58](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:58), [pdf-read.ts:272](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:272), [pdf-read.ts:1140](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:1140), [concurrency.ts:270](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/concurrency.ts:270), [pdf-read.ts:865](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:865).

- **F5 — P1: “No more quality notes” is not a sufficient quality gate.** Chunk boundaries affect the checker’s overall score, thin-page search, deduplication, and recovery behavior, so note counts from different plans are not directly equivalent. The code’s own rationale says 1,600 words was previously rejected because it made eleven chunks—and eleven opportunities for failure. Compare page-level recall/coverage, structural recoveries and refusals, total attempts, and seam behavior for paragraphs, headings, lists, equations, tables, and continuation markers. Smaller chunks may reduce summarising drift, but increase independent formatting decisions and failure opportunities. Evidence: [plan:59](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/docs/plans/261011u-more-pdf-shards-faster.md:59), [pdf-read.ts:171](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:171), [pdf-score.ts:895](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-score.ts:895), [pdf-score.ts:960](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-score.ts:960), [pdf-read.ts:3506](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:3506).

- **F6 — P2: Two text-layer documents do not cover a global default change.** Scans have no usable word counts, while image-heavy pages are governed by the byte/page bounds. Smaller page limits therefore affect them differently from Kuhn or Doctorow. Before Stage 3, include one scan or figure-heavy PDF—or explicitly limit the conclusion to text-layer PDFs. Evidence: [pdf-read.ts:576](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:576), [pdf-read.ts:579](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:579).

- **F7 — P2: `nullCheckpointStore()` correctly prevents application replay, but does not control upstream warming.** Repeated identical PDFs may benefit from provider prompt or file-processing caches, while the first arm may also bear local module warm-up. Balance run order and record cache-token telemetry; do not infer “cold” merely from disabled checkpoints. Conversely, checkpoint-free runs answer fresh-upload speed, not production retry/resume speed. Evidence: [pdf-read.ts:4253](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/pdf-read.ts:4253), [schema.ts:4046](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbz3uz86-pdf-shard-concurrency/src/db/schema.ts:4046).

- **F8 — P2: An isolated-call pilot is cheaper, but the existing spike is not an end-to-end substitute.** It uses 32,000 output tokens rather than production’s 16,000 and bypasses the production gate, transport retries, checks, re-asks, recovery, and post-fold. Use the production reader on representative matched chunks as an early rejection test; only run the full matrix for candidates whose raw tail latency improves enough to overcome additional waves. Then confirm through real `runPdfExtract`. Measure peak RSS and test a plan over 100 chunks, not just one ordinary fixture. Changing boundaries is correctness-safe because checkpoint keys include pages and context, but existing in-progress checkpoints for old ranges will miss and be repurchased after deployment.
