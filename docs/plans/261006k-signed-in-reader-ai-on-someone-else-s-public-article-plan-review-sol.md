1. **P1 — Question 2 uses Quick-search economics to justify option D, where they do not apply.** A Quick search costs per provider call/chunk, not a flat $0.0004 per search. Production averaged $0.0006 per call, long articles use several calls, and typing may start several searches ([search.md](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbdy-ai-on-public-article-signed-in/docs/project/search.md:358)). Meaning averaged $0.059 per call, while Chat and several other paid actions have no general account limit ([routes.ts](/var/tmp/spideryarn-worktrees/fbdy-ai-on-public-article-signed-in/src/routes.ts:9605)). Therefore “50 searches” and “a few dollars a day at worst” do not bound D. Change Question 2 into two decisions: for C, choose a per-person Quick allowance plus an atomic global cost fuse; for D, require the general monetary/token spend limit before deciding entitlement. Do not describe either ceiling using `$0.0004 × button presses`.

Change lines 40–45, options A/B, and Question 1 accordingly.

They can share route conventions, but not one lookup “built once and used twice.” Remove that sequencing argument.

   No small, no-defence implementation can invoke a server model **in place** for a non-owner; B duplicates the article, while the Words option above invokes no model.

6. **P2 — The plan needs an explicit security contract for C and D.** It should require that:
