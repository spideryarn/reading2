On [your profile](/profile), **About you** tells the AI who is reading: your background, what you
know well, what you are rusty on. It is used on every article, and it changes *what gets explained,
and how much* — which terms the glossary thinks you need, how chat pitches an answer. It never
changes what the article says. The glossary is where you will notice it most.

Each article also asks **Why are you reading this?** while it is being added, and saves your answer
as you type; change it later on its Metadata page. Something like “I want the methods, not the
history” is what helps — the quiz, for one, aims more of its questions at what you said.

- These boxes save themselves a moment after you stop typing.
- Text written with your profile has a small **person icon** in the corner of its mode; point at it
  to see what it means, or press it to read and edit your profile there. When it marks text written
  for an older profile, the icon gains a pencil and a warmer colour. **Nothing is rewritten by
  itself**. The panel offers to replace the result where the mode can do that; Quotes is the
  exception, because its control adds to the list instead. Even a one-letter fix counts as an edit.
- The command bar can turn your reason for reading into a short list of things to try: [Suggest what
  to do here](/help/keyboard).
- The article’s parts and sections are never personalised, so they are the same for everyone.
- To stop the AI using a profile at all, empty both boxes.
