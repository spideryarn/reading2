The spine is the thin strip down the left edge: the whole article squeezed to the height of your
window. It is **proportional** — a part that fills half the piece fills half the spine — so it is a
picture of the article’s shape, not a list of headings. At a glance it shows how far through you
are, how big each part is, and where the things you are looking for sit.

What each mark means:

- **Tinted blocks, stacked top to bottom: the parts.** Each is as tall as that part is long, with a
  thin line between one and the next. A short part can be a sliver too thin to see.
- **One block a little brighter: the part you are in.**
- **The strongest fill: the section you are in**, inside the brighter part. It does not appear when
  a part has no sections.
- **Short hairlines inside a part: where its sections begin**, even where there is no room to name
  them.
- **Very faint blocks near the bottom: endnotes, references and other back matter.** Dimmed so you
  can see where the argument ends, but kept at their true size. If the spine says you are 60%
  through and the rest is faint, you have nearly finished the article itself.
- **A box with orange top and bottom edges: what is on your screen.** It moves as you scroll, and
  its height shows how much of the article fits on one screen.
- **A tinted area from the left edge, with a brighter line at its edge: where you have spent time
  reading.** The further across the strip it reaches, the longer you spent there. A quick glance may
  leave no shading. Use it to find where you had got to after jumping around. Only whoever added the
  article sees it.
- **One faint grey bar across the full width: where you jumped from.** It appears with the **↩ back
  to …** button and goes when that does — see [Jumping around](/help/jumping-around).
- **Coloured marks down the right edge: the passages the open mode points at.** In Search, each
  search you have switched on gets its own narrow lane in its own colour; in Quotes, Ideas, Skim and
  the other modes that point at passages, the marks are those passages. **Each mark is as tall as
  its paragraph**, so you can see how a set of results is spread: bunched in one section, scattered
  evenly, or only in the notes. A list of results cannot show you that.

**Point at the spine to read it.** Every section has a card. It starts with a small outline of the
article showing where the section sits: the part it belongs to, the sections on either side, and its
place in the part (“3 of 7”) when the part is long. Under the section’s own name are a short gist
and what is inside it. Then come how many words it has, how far in it starts, and how many search
matches it holds. A heading set in the author’s typeface is the author’s own; one in the AI’s was
written by the AI. Once a card is open, move slowly down the spine and the cards follow, so you can
read the outline section by section.

**Click to go there.** A click lands at the start of that section. On a touchscreen, the first tap
opens the card and the second goes there, because most sections are too thin to tap blind. With the
pointer over the spine, <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move one part at a time.
