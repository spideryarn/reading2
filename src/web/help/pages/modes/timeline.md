## When to use it

On history, reporting and narrative pieces. Every row is the article’s own claim about time, kept as
firm or as vague as the article left it — not when things really happened. On an argument or a
review it will often find nothing, and says so rather than padding; trying again would find the same
nothing.

## Reading it

- **A date**, such as *26 May* or *at or before 12 May*, was read from the article’s own words by
  plain code, not by the AI.
- **Words in quotation marks**, such as *“another month later”*, are the article’s own phrase — as
  precise as the piece ever got.
- **A dash** means the piece gives no time for this at all.
- Where the article gives a day and month but no year, the year comes from when the piece was
  published, and the list says so.

Events the piece only raises as possibilities (**What might have happened**) and things it expects
(**What the piece expects**) sit in groups of their own below. With fewer than three events, the
panel tells you the piece is not really telling a story in time. Press a row to see how the piece
dates it and jump there in the text.
