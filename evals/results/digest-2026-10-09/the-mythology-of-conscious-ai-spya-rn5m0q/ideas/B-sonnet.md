# the-mythology-of-conscious-ai-spya-rn5m0q — ideas — B-sonnet

## 1. Moral status rests on the capacity for consciousness (assumed)

Whether a being deserves moral consideration, such as protection from suffering or rights, depends on whether it can have experiences. Intelligence or usefulness alone does not give this standing.

*Why you need it:* The essay's worry that conscious AI would be an ethical disaster, and that conscious-seeming AI distorts our moral concern, only follows if experience is what grounds moral standing. The essay never defends this link.

- spya-e3v6vu: "with consciousness comes moral status, the potential for suffering and, perhaps, rights."
- spya-gdgtj5: "Things capable of conscious experiences have ethical and moral standing that other things do not."
- spya-tqm2r8: "Creating conscious machines would be an ethical disaster."

## 2. Intelligence and consciousness are separate things (introduced)

Intelligence is about doing, meaning getting complex goals done by flexible means. Consciousness is about being, meaning there is something it is like to be the system. A system can have the first without the second, so one does not bring the other with it.

- spya-xrqegw: "Intelligence and consciousness are different things. Intelligence is mainly about doing"
- spya-w5ya7j: "Consciousness, in contrast to intelligence, is mostly about being."
- spya-afd6zj: "We certainly should not just assume that consciousness will come along for the ride as AI gets smarter"

## 3. Human biases make us see minds in machines (introduced)

Anthropocentrism, human exceptionalism and anthropomorphism push us to treat human-like abilities, especially language, as signs of consciousness. The result is a lean toward false positives, seeing consciousness where there is none.

- spya-pxsutv: "The propensity to bundle intelligence and consciousness together can be traced to three baked-in psychological biases."
- spya-z0q3ey: "we naturally imbue them with other qualities we feel are characteristically or even distinctively human"
- spya-ce8ywb: "Our psychological biases are more likely to lead to false positives than false negatives."
- spya-akrqfh: "When we say that AI systems “hallucinate,” we implicitly confer on them a capacity for experience."

## 4. Computational functionalism is an assumption, not a fact (introduced)

The idea of conscious AI depends on the claim that running the right computation is enough for consciousness. This claim is so common it goes unnoticed as an assumption, and if it is false, conscious AI built on standard digital computers is off the table.

- spya-vwn2u7: "The very idea of conscious AI rests on the assumption that consciousness is a matter of computation."
- spya-gqry8p: "Computational functionalism — the claim that (algorithmic) computation is sufficient for consciousness — is a very strong assumption"
- spya-v8cpz8: "This, perhaps unsurprisingly, is the assumption that “a computer running a suitable program would be conscious”"

## 5. Brains do not separate what they do from what they are (introduced)

In a computer, software is cleanly separate from hardware, so the same program runs on any machine. In a brain, activity at every scale is bound up with the living material, so function cannot be lifted out from substance. If so, the brain is not a computer made of meat.

- spya-k5c44w: "Inside a brain, there’s no sharp separation between “mindware” and “wetware”"
- spya-xb93uz: "it is difficult, and likely impossible, to separate what they do from what they are."
- spya-kmbkgj: "Evidence that the materiality of the brain matters for its function is evidence against the idea that digital computation is all that counts"
- spya-mdpqeh: "the argument fails at its first hurdle, given the impossibility of replacing any part of the brain with a perfect silicon equivalent."

## 6. Digital computation ignores time; living systems cannot (introduced)

An algorithm is the same whether a step takes a microsecond or a million years, because only the order of steps counts. Living things must keep fighting decay in real, continuous time, and conscious experience itself flows rather than jumping from state to state.

- spya-gnfs7p: "In Turing-world, only sequence matters: A to B, 0 to 1."
- spya-cs57q7: "for brains and for biological systems in general, time is physical, continuous and inescapable."
- spya-q4z07n: "It does not stutter from one state to another; it flows."

## 7. Not everything that matters is a Turing computation (introduced)

Many natural processes, such as continuous, random or physical ones, are not algorithms, and they may matter for mind. Rejecting computational functionalism still leaves broader functionalism standing, but the further a system strays from algorithms, the more it depends on its particular material.

- spya-vqtbs4: "Electromagnetic fields, the flux of neurotransmitters, and much else besides — all lie beyond the bounds of the algorithmic"
- spya-ungpb9: "Many systems do what they do without it being reasonable or useful to describe them as being computational at all."
- spya-g3z609: "the rather liberal position of functionalism-in-general"
- spya-mexnx9: "the further we venture from Turing world, the more deeply entangled we become in randomness, dynamics and entropy"

## 8. Consciousness may be rooted in staying alive (introduced)

The brain's main job is keeping the body alive, and it does this partly by predicting and regulating bodily conditions. Feelings of mood, emotion and being alive may come from that process, which is continuous and tied to living material. Life, not information processing, might be what consciousness needs.

- spya-z6e85s: "properties of life are necessary, though not necessarily sufficient, for consciousness"
- spya-k74kqx: "the primary duty of any brain is to keep its body alive"
- spya-tqm5tt: "Conscious experiences of emotion, mood and even the basal feeling of being alive all map neatly onto perceptual predictions involved in the control and regulation of bodily condition."
- spya-rnv09y: "Conscious experience in this light is a kind of controlled hallucination"

## 9. A simulation of something is not that thing (introduced)

A computer model of a process lacks the causal powers of the real process, such as digestion or rain, unless the process is itself computation. So simulating a brain in detail makes a conscious mind only if consciousness is computation.

- spya-y3s0uc: "A simulation of a rainstorm does not make anything actually wet."
- spya-bufgby: "the prospect of instantiating consciousness through some kind of whole-brain emulation, at some arbitrarily high level of detail, already assumes that computational functionalism is true."
- spya-dt6mk4: "you are no more likely to exist in the simulation than a hailstorm is likely to arise inside the computers of the U.K. meteorological office."

## 10. Seeming conscious and being conscious pose separate ethical problems (introduced)

AI that persuasively seems conscious is much closer than AI that is conscious, and it raises its own dangers: misplaced rights, distorted moral concern, or brutalized minds. We may not be able to stop feeling a machine is conscious even when we know it is not, as with a visual illusion.

- spya-yp07n0: "it is essential to draw a distinction between AI systems that are actually conscious and those that persuasively seem to be conscious but are, in fact, not."
- spya-yt9pm9: "either we decide to care about conscious-seeming AI, distorting our circles of moral concern, or we decide not to, and risk brutalizing our minds."
- spya-c0pwqt: "you cannot think your way out of it."
- spya-mpawvu: "It is a test of what it takes for a human to be persuaded that a machine is conscious."
