# the-mythology-of-conscious-ai-spya-rn5m0q — ideas — A-opus

## 1. A conscious being's interests limit what its makers may do (assumed)

If something can genuinely experience, it has a claim not to be harmed or ended at our convenience. Shutting down a truly conscious machine would be closer to killing than to switching off an appliance.

*Why you need it:* The warning that granting rights would stop us turning AI off assumes that rights for a feeling being include protection from being ended. Without that, rights and the off-switch would not clash.

- spya-qrkt96: "And if we do endow our AI creations with rights, we may not be able to turn them off, even if they act against our interests."
- spya-tqm2r8: "And if we give these systems rights, as arguably we should if they really are conscious, we will hamper our ability to control them, or to shut them down if we need to."
- spya-yt9pm9: "we might give AI systems “rights” that they don’t actually need, since they would not actually be conscious, restricting our ability to control them for no good reason."

## 2. We judge other minds by how much they resemble known ones (assumed)

We have no direct access to another being's experience. We credit it with consciousness by comparing it with the beings we already accept as conscious. That comparison is only as good as our choice of which resemblances really matter.

*Why you need it:* Several steps lean on this. 'Everything agreed to be conscious is alive' is evidence only if shared features support the inference. AI is said to resemble us in the wrong ways. And the question becomes how brain-like a system must be. All of these presuppose that consciousness is attributed by relevant similarity.

- spya-z6e85s: "every candidate for consciousness that most people currently agree on as actually being conscious is also alive."
- spya-ce8ywb: "Compared to non-human animals, the apparent wonders of AI may be more similar to us in ways that do not matter for consciousness, like linguistic ability, and less similar in ways that do, like being alive."
- spya-qzwr7t: "AlphaFold, which predicts protein structure rather than words, just doesn’t pull our psychological strings in the same way."
- spya-cypt9v: "the only things most people currently agree are conscious are made of meat, not metal."

## 3. Intelligence is doing, consciousness is being (introduced)

Intelligence is the ability to reach complex goals by flexible means, a matter of what a system can do. Consciousness is whether there is something it feels like to be that system. One can exist without the other, so getting smarter does not make a machine more likely to feel anything.

- spya-xrqegw: "A useful general definition of intelligence is the ability to achieve complex goals by flexible means."
- spya-w5ya7j: "Consciousness, in contrast to intelligence, is mostly about being."
- spya-h58fmt: "But just because they go together in us doesn’t mean that they go together in general."
- spya-afd6zj: "We certainly should not just assume that consciousness will come along for the ride as AI gets smarter"

## 4. Conscious AI rests on an unexamined computational assumption (introduced)

The hope for conscious AI depends on computational functionalism: the claim that running the right kind of computation is enough, on its own, to produce consciousness. This is an assumption rather than an established fact, and many theories and arguments simply take it for granted.

*Why you need it:* Every later argument is aimed at this single premise. If it falls, conscious AI of the familiar digital kind falls with it.

- spya-vwn2u7: "This assumption, which philosophers call computational functionalism, is so deeply ingrained that it can be difficult to recognize it as an assumption at all."
- spya-v8cpz8: "That this strong assumption is taken on board without examination in a philosophical discussion that is all about the validity of assumptions is yet another indication of how deeply ingrained the computational view of mind and brain has become."
- spya-pyy07g: "They also generally sidestep defending computational functionalism, being content instead to assume it."

## 5. In brains, what they do cannot be separated from what they are (introduced)

Computers are designed so that software can run unchanged on different hardware. Brains have no such split. A neuron's function is bound up with its chemistry and the energy processes that keep it alive, so you cannot simply swap its job onto another material.

*Why you need it:* This is the reason the familiar thought experiment of replacing neurons one by one with silicon parts does not work, and the reason the brain cannot be treated as a program running on meat.

- spya-xb93uz: "brains are the kinds of things for which it is difficult, and likely impossible, to separate what they do from what they are."
- spya-wwktbr: "The only way to seamlessly replace a biological neuron is with another biological neuron — and ideally, the same one."
- spya-mdpqeh: "But the argument fails at its first hurdle, given the impossibility of replacing any part of the brain with a perfect silicon equivalent."
- spya-kmbkgj: "Evidence that the materiality of the brain matters for its function is evidence against the idea that digital computation is all that counts, which in turn is evidence against computational functionalism."

## 6. Algorithms care about order; life runs in real time (introduced)

An algorithm is defined only by the sequence of its steps. It would be the same computation whether each step took a microsecond or a million years. Living systems, by contrast, must keep working continuously against decay, so time is physically built into what they do. Experience itself also seems to flow rather than jump from state to state.

- spya-gnfs7p: "In Turing-world, only sequence matters: A to B, 0 to 1."
- spya-cs57q7: "Computation exists out of time, but computers do not."
- spya-q4z07n: "It does not stutter from one state to another; it flows."

## 7. Functional organization need not mean computation (introduced)

Functionalism in general says the mind depends on how a system is organized and what it does. That organization could include continuous physical dynamics, chemistry or bodily processes. Computational functionalism is a much narrower claim: that only step-by-step algorithmic computation counts. Rejecting the narrow claim still leaves the broad one standing.

- spya-g3z609: "a tendency to conflate the rather liberal position of functionalism-in-general, since functional organization can include many things, with the very specific claim of computational functionalism"
- spya-mexnx9: "The question is no longer about which algorithms give rise to consciousness; it’s about how brain-like a system has to be to move the needle on its potential to be conscious."
- spya-ungpb9: "A “computational governor,” sensing engine speed, calculating the necessary actions and then sending precise motor signals to switch actuators on or off, would not only be hopelessly inefficient but would betray a total misunderstanding of what’s really going on."

## 8. Experience is the brain's best guess, anchored in keeping the body alive (introduced)

The brain does not read the world directly. What we perceive is its running prediction of what is causing its sensory signals, corrected by those signals: a 'controlled hallucination'. The deepest of these predictions concern the body's own internal state, serving the job of staying alive. That is a possible reason why consciousness may need life, not just information processing.

- spya-rnv09y: "Conscious experience in this light is a kind of controlled hallucination"
- spya-k74kqx: "the primary duty of any brain is to keep its body alive"
- spya-tqm5tt: "Conscious experiences of emotion, mood and even the basal feeling of being alive all map neatly onto perceptual predictions involved in the control and regulation of bodily condition."
- spya-tm06bg: "the ubiquitous process of prediction error minimization becomes inseparable from the materiality of life itself."

## 9. Simulating something does not create it (introduced)

A computer model of X reproduces X only if X is itself a computation. A simulated rainstorm wets nothing. So even a perfectly detailed brain simulation would be conscious only if computational functionalism is already true. And the fact that something can be simulated does not show that it is computational.

- spya-y3s0uc: "the fact that X can be simulated computationally does not justify the conclusion that X is itself computational."
- spya-bufgby: "the prospect of instantiating consciousness through some kind of whole-brain emulation, at some arbitrarily high level of detail, already assumes that computational functionalism is true."
- spya-dt6mk4: "If an exquisitely detailed brain model is needed, then you are no more likely to exist in the simulation than a hailstorm is likely to arise inside the computers of the U.K. meteorological office."

## 10. Seeming conscious is its own problem, separate from being conscious (introduced)

AI that only appears conscious is much closer than AI that is, and it raises different dangers. Either we extend care where none is needed, or we harden ourselves by mistreating things that seem to feel. And knowing better may not dispel the impression, just as knowing two lines are equal does not stop one looking longer.

- spya-yp07n0: "it is essential to draw a distinction between AI systems that are actually conscious and those that persuasively seem to be conscious but are, in fact, not."
- spya-yt9pm9: "either we decide to care about conscious-seeming AI, distorting our circles of moral concern, or we decide not to, and risk brutalizing our minds."
- spya-c0pwqt: "Illusions of artificial consciousness might be as impenetrable to our minds as some visual illusions."
- spya-mpawvu: "It is a test of what it takes for a human to be persuaded that a machine is conscious."
