# the-mythology-of-conscious-ai-spya-rn5m0q — ideas — D-haiku

## 1. Input-output behaviour is taken to capture what a part does (assumed)

A part's function can be fully captured by the mapping from its inputs to its outputs, so a silicon stand-in that matches that mapping would count as a faithful replacement.

*Why you need it:* The replacement thought experiment only supports the claim that silicon could take over brain function if matching input-output behaviour is enough to preserve what a neuron does. The article's objection is that a neuron also does metabolic work, which the mapping leaves out.

- spya-mdpqeh: "invites us to imagine progressively replacing brain parts with silicon equivalents that function in exactly the same way as their biological counterparts."
- spya-xpydj4: "you cannot assume it is possible to replace a single biological neuron with a silicon equivalent, while leaving its function, its input-output behavior, perfectly preserved."

## 2. Consciousness is about being, intelligence about doing (introduced)

Intelligence is the ability to get complex goals done by flexible means, which is a matter of what a system does. Consciousness is whether there is something it is like to be that system, which is a separate matter from what it does.

- spya-xrqegw: "Intelligence is mainly about doing: solving a crossword puzzle, assembling some furniture, navigating a tricky family situation, walking to the shop — all involve intelligent behavior of some kind."
- spya-w5ya7j: "Consciousness, in contrast to intelligence, is mostly about being."

## 3. Brain software and hardware cannot be cleanly separated (introduced)

In a computer, the program can be moved to different hardware without changing what it does. In a brain, what it does is bound up with what it is made of, so the two cannot be pulled apart.

- spya-k5c44w: "Inside a brain, there’s no sharp separation between “mindware” and “wetware” as there is between software and hardware in a computer."
- spya-xb93uz: "Unlike computers, even computers running neural network algorithms, brains are the kinds of things for which it is difficult, and likely impossible, to separate what they do from what they are."

## 4. A simulation is not the thing it simulates (introduced)

A computer model of a process reproduces the process's pattern but not its causal powers, so simulating something does not bring it into being unless the thing itself is computational.

- spya-y3s0uc: "In general, a computational simulation of X does not bring X into being — does not instantiate X — unless X is a computational process (specifically, an algorithm) itself."
- spya-bufgby: "A computational simulation of the brain (and body), however detailed it may be, will only give rise to consciousness if consciousness is a matter of computation."

## 5. Seeming-conscious AI is its own ethical problem (introduced)

Machines that seem conscious raise ethical problems even if they are not conscious. Those problems are distinct from the ones raised by machines that really are conscious, so the two should not be merged.

- spya-yp07n0: "When it comes to the impact of AI in society, it is essential to draw a distinction between AI systems that are actually conscious and those that persuasively seem to be conscious but are, in fact, not."
- spya-yt9pm9: "either we decide to care about conscious-seeming AI, distorting our circles of moral concern, or we decide not to, and risk brutalizing our minds."
