# the-mythology-of-conscious-ai-spya-rn5m0q — chat-q2 — C-sonnet+digest

> What does Seth mean when he says a simulation is not an instantiation? Is that convincing? What would a functionalist say back, and does the essay answer that reply or only assert its own view?

He means that modeling something is not the same as making it: "A simulation of the digestive system does not actually digest anything," and a simulated rainstorm gets nothing wet [spya-y3s0uc]. There is one built-in exception: simulating X does create X if X is itself a computation [spya-y3s0uc]. So a detailed brain simulation would be conscious only if consciousness is a matter of computation [spya-bufgby].

My inference is that the principle is convincing but does almost no work on its own. A functionalist agrees with the principle and places consciousness in the exception, like a simulated chess game, which is a chess game. Their reply would be that consciousness depends on how a system is organized, so a simulation that reproduces the organization reproduces the consciousness. The essay half-concedes this: simulation fails "unless computational functionalism happens anyway to be true" [spya-ksbt2g]. Calling the point "obvious and uncontroversial for consciousness, too" [spya-bufgby] overreaches, because the disagreement is exactly whether consciousness is digestion-like or chess-like. This is also why the claim that each argument "can stand up by itself" [spya-v8e0gy] fits this one poorly.

The essay doesn't rebut the reply at this point. It leans on the earlier sections instead:

- **Brains and computers:** brains can't cleanly separate what they do from what they are [spya-xb93uz], and a perfect silicon neuron replacement is said to be impossible [spya-mdpqeh].
- **Continuous and random processes:** they lie outside Turing computation, though they can be approximated [spya-e5kxct].
- **Life:** Seth admits he has no "knock-down argument" here [spya-z6e85s].

The article doesn't say whether a close enough approximation might be good enough, which is where a functionalist would press. He also grants that rival theories simply assume functionalism, and that he may be wrong [spya-pyy07g]. So the essay gives reasons to doubt the functionalist premise but does not refute it.
