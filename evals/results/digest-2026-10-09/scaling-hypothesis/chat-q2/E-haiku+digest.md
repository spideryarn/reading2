> What does Gwern mean when he says agency can come from data, and what is the weakest step in his argument that a model trained only to predict text can end up behaving as an agent?

Gwern means that a model trained only to predict data can learn to model agents well enough to act like one, and that this could happen even without agent-like data. The first route is human text. Such text is full of people pursuing goals, so a model that predicts it learns generative models of many agents, which can be prompted to plan and roleplay [spya-cw7jg4]. He writes that "a sufficiently accurate simulation of an agent just is an agent" [spya-nkzwpu]. The second route is non-agent data, such as river hydrology or physics. Here he argues that agency is a continuum and that treating a system as "wanting" to minimise something is a cheap shortcut a network might adopt [spya-mkbrjp] [spya-ehe426] [spya-agstbd]. He hedges this heavily, calling it "maybe not even that small" a possibility [spya-ueb3d2] and saying whether it happens is "an empirical matter" [spya-nxp8hh].

The weakest step, in my view, is the move from modelling an agent to being one. The "just is an agent" claim [spya-nkzwpu] is asserted rather than argued. The real-world cases he cites, such as a language model guiding a robot in SayCan, depend on effectors being attached to the model [spya-cw7jg4]. He also notes that people hook models up to browsers, APIs and robots as soon as they can [spya-fm6pfc]. So the agent-like behaviour in those cases comes from the wiring, not from prediction alone. This is my inference, not something the article states outright. The second route is weaker still, since he concedes that agency-like shortcuts must "pay their way" against simpler approximations [spya-dbrxu8].
