# scaling-hypothesis — chat-q2 — B-sonnet

> What does Gwern mean when he says agency can come from data, and what is the weakest step in his argument that a model trained only to predict text can end up behaving as an agent?

Gwern means that a model trained only to predict human text has to learn what the people in that text would do, and so can act as them when prompted. He calls GPT-3's training "offline imitation learning (behavioral cloning)" from text by real and fictional agents, which gives it generative models of many agents. Prompting it to "roleplay" then gets plans that steer toward goals [spya-cw7jg4]. His stated rule is that "a sufficiently accurate simulation of an agent just is an agent" [spya-nkzwpu]. He then stretches the claim: agency is "a continuum," useful even for understanding non-agentic things, so it can emerge without agent data [spya-ueb3d2]. His example is that we treat a river as "wanting" to minimise its height, and a net would find that shortcut attractive [spya-ehe426 spya-agstbd].

In my judgement, the weakest step is that stretch from imitating agents to developing agency from non-agent data. He grants that it is "an empirical matter" and that we can't say what would trigger it [spya-nxp8hh]. He also grants that the variational shortcut is unlikely to be learned for a single system like Pluto [spya-dbrxu8]. The Turing-machine argument, which is meant to show there is "no escape," rests on a chain of maybes. A model inferring a random machine's rules is likened to a rudimentary "theory of mind," and the text answers its own question with "Maybe" [spya-usu3q6]. My inference is that this slides from "the model represents what an agent would do" to "the model is an agent," and the article asserts that equivalence rather than argues for it.

The conclusion is also hard to test. He says sampling "can show the presence of knowledge, but not the absence," so no check could show agency is absent [spya-jrm0w9]. That makes "tampering with data is not a solution" [spya-hjpau9] difficult to falsify.
