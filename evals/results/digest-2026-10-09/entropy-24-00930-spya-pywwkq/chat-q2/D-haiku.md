> What exactly is 'synergy' in PID, and how far should I trust the finding that synergy concentrates in rich clubs and recurrent connections? What could the choice of redundancy measure, or the amount of data, do to that result?

**Synergy** is the part of what two input neurons jointly tell you about a target's future that neither input tells you alone. The whole (joint information) is compared with the sum of the parts, and when the whole is larger, the extra is synergy [spya-e94ury]. With two parents, it is the information visible only in their joint state [spya-h5jzvm]. The article treats synergy as a measure of processing, since the target is combining its inputs rather than just passing one along [spya-sp50v3].

On trust: the rich-club and recurrent-connection findings come from one group's analyses of cultured cortical slices. The rich-club result is strong on its face: rich-club triads had 2.7 times the synergy of other triads [spya-fud8q3]. But the networks were built with bivariate transfer entropy, which the authors note can overestimate significant edges [spya-j4cy9j]. The recurrent result is a regression pattern, not an experiment [spya-sd9fzd]. Recurrent connections also come with extra connectivity around rich clubs, so the two findings may partly be one finding. My inference is that the rich-club and recurrence results are correlational and should be treated as hypotheses. I have not checked whether others have replicated or challenged them, so that is unverified here.

**Redundancy measure.** Synergy is calculated as what remains after redundancy is estimated, so the choice of redundancy function changes the synergy numbers directly. The article says there are close to a dozen competing measures, each with trade-offs, and that most of the studies used Imin, which has been criticised for unintuitive behaviour [spya-rc76qn]. My inference is that a ranking such as "rich-club triads are more synergistic" might survive a different measure, but the 2.7× figure might not.

**Amount of data.** Estimating the joint distribution of k neurons requires about 2^k configurations, so undersampling can badly distort the estimates [spya-tz99zn]. The plug-in estimator tends to overestimate mutual information [spya-qa7gt2]. The article says this is less of a concern for single-neuron binary recordings with long recording times, which is closer to the setting here [spya-qa7gt2]. Using many bins for continuous data would make it worse [spya-uktkdy].
