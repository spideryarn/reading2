> What exactly is 'synergy' in PID, and how far should I trust the finding that synergy concentrates in rich clubs and recurrent connections? What could the choice of redundancy measure, or the amount of data, do to that result?

Synergy is the information about the target neuron that you get only by knowing both source neurons' states together, not from either one alone [spya-h5jzvm]. The article's test is that the joint mutual information exceeds the sum of the individual ones [spya-e94ury]. It cannot be measured directly: there are three measurable quantities and four unknown parts, so you must first choose a formula for redundancy, and the rest follow from it [spya-bgsd4n]. The authors also concede that this is a statistical dependency, not a mechanism [spya-spdx0y].

**How far to trust the rich-club and recurrent findings.** The numbers are rich-club triads with 2.7 times the synergy and about 88% of network-wide synergy [spya-fud8q3], and triads with two recurrent connections with 50% more synergy [spya-sd9fzd]. The authors flag two caveats themselves. The networks came from pairwise transfer entropy, which overstates connections, and the data were cultured slices, not behaving animals [spya-j4cy9j]. The monkey study partly answers this: with a stricter network method, it reproduced the rich club and higher synergy in highly connected nodes [spya-wr7f6y]. The article does not say the recurrent-connection result was tested there. My inference is that the findings are strongest as qualitative patterns, and weaker as exact magnitudes.

**Redundancy measure.** No measure is universally accepted, and about a dozen compete. Most of the studies here used the original Imin, which has been criticized for unintuitive behavior [spya-rc76qn]. The article reports no check that the results hold under other measures. My inference is that because synergy is what remains after redundancy is subtracted, a measure that counts less redundancy would raise synergy. Comparisons like rich club versus non-rich club may survive that, but figures like 2.7× or "about a quarter of transfer entropy" [spya-kqkb58] may not.

**Amount of data.** The simple estimator overestimates mutual information, so unrelated signals can look dependent [spya-qa7gt2]. The authors say this matters less for binary spike data with very long recordings, but grows as more variables are involved [spya-qa7gt2 spya-tz99zn]. Triads involve only three variables, so this is probably a modest worry here. My inference is that sparse or low-rate neurons are where bias could creep in, and the null-model comparisons the authors recommend are the safeguard [spya-qa7gt2].
