> Why do the authors say that mutual information and transfer entropy are not enough to describe information processing in neurons, and how does that motivation connect to what the empirical PID studies in section 5 actually found?

The authors' argument is that mutual information and transfer entropy describe how information moves between neurons, not how it gets combined. Mutual information is undirected and instantaneous, so it has no notion of "transfer" or "flow" [spya-f6sbgx]. Transfer entropy fixes that by measuring how much a source's past predicts a target's future, which makes it a measure of propagation [spya-p4pyuy]. But the article says transfer is limited in showing how different streams "interact" and how neurons produce novel or modified information from several sources [spya-s0db2g]. "Processing" is therefore defined as how two sources' pasts jointly influence the target in a way that isn't reducible to either alone [spya-wer4cd]. That is the synergy term of PID, which the article also calls "information modification" [spya-e94ury].

Section 5 connects to this in two ways. First, propagation and processing turn out to be related but not identical. Synergy tracked transfer entropy across triads, at about a quarter of it in these recordings [spya-kqkb58]. That is the basis for testing rich clubs, where synergy was 2.7 times higher per triad [spya-fud8q3], and for the finding that more connections between the sources go with more synergy [spya-sd9fzd].

Second, the clearest case where flow alone would mislead is the timescale result. Total transfer entropy kept rising as time bins lengthened, but synergy rose only to a point and then fell, while redundancy kept climbing [spya-cs9fdy] [spya-ybmve2]. The monkey study is similar. Activity was mostly synergy-dominated, but redundancy rose relatively during movement, which the authors interpret as a task demand [spya-t8fayf].

My inference is that the strong synergy–transfer entropy correlation cuts both ways. It supports the claim that PID adds something beyond flow only where the two diverge, as in the timescale result. Also, the authors concede that PID gives statistical dependencies, not a mechanism [spya-spdx0y], so equating synergy with "computation" is a definition they adopt [spya-e94ury], not something the experiments show.
