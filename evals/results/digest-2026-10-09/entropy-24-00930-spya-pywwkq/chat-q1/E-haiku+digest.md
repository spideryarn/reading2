> Why do the authors say that mutual information and transfer entropy are not enough to describe information processing in neurons, and how does that motivation connect to what the empirical PID studies in section 5 actually found?

The authors say mutual information and transfer entropy show that information moves between neurons, but not how neurons combine the streams they receive. Mutual information is "an undirected measure that describes the instantaneous dependency between two variables" with no sense of direction [spya-f6sbgx]. Transfer entropy adds direction, measuring how much a source's past predicts a target's next state [spya-p4pyuy]. Even so, the authors say transfer "is limited in its ability to reveal how different streams of information 'interact' and how neurons produce novel or modified information from multiple sources" [spya-s0db2g]. PID is their proposed tool for that, because it isolates the part of the target's information that "is not reducible to either source considered individually" [spya-wer4cd].

Section 5 takes this as its starting point, though the results mostly tie synergy back to flow. Synergy in triads was "reliably about a quarter of the transfer entropy for that triad," which the authors read as feedforward propagation being "a reliable predictor of information processing" [spya-kqkb58]. The motivation contrasts flow with combination, but this finding links them. My inference is that the two measures are not fully independent, since synergy is a part of the information the sources jointly give the target. The article does not discuss this. The later results add structure: synergy rises with recurrent connections [spya-sd9fzd] and peaks at moderate input similarity before redundancy takes over [spya-ybmve2]. In the monkey study, the balance of synergy and redundancy shifts with the task [spya-t8fayf].
