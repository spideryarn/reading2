> **Reader 2:** The link is meant to be that prediction error minimisation is bottomed out in metabolism, so the inference is tied to staying alive.

**Tutor 2** — 137 words, 1 question mark, 2 citations

That's the mechanism — he spells out the full convergence: perceptual predictions about bodily condition map onto feelings, those processes are rooted in life as "self-regenerating storms of life resisting the pull of entropic sameness," and third, "all of this is non-computational, or at least non-algorithmic" [spya-da9tvt].

That third step is the one worth pressure-testing, given your field: predictive processing is usually formalized as Bayesian inference — minimizing a free-energy functional, which is itself a mathematical, implementable scheme. Seth wants the *biological implementation* of prediction-error minimization to be non-algorithmic because it's "inseparable from its material basis" [spya-da9tvt], not the inference scheme itself.

Where would you push back — is there a real difference between "PP as formal theory" and "PP as implemented in metabolism," or is he trading on an ambiguity in what counts as "computational" here?

> **Reader 3:** I think the weak point is that you could implement the same predictive dynamics in a non-living system.

**Tutor 3** — 127 words, 1 question mark, 3 citations

That's the objection the article's own structure anticipates, since argument 4 — simulation is not instantiation — exists partly to block it. Seth's position: if "the same predictive dynamics" means the same formal scheme running on different hardware, that's simulation, and "a computational simulation of X does not bring X into being... unless X is a computational process... itself" [spya-npjt4j]. His claim about PP specifically is that prediction-error minimization in real brains is "non-computational, or at least non-algorithmic" [spya-da9tvt], inseparable from the self-producing, metabolic turnover of living tissue [spya-vys3vj].

So the question back to you: is that claim — that biological PEM is non-algorithmic because tied to metabolism — argued for independently, or is it just asserted and then used to rule out your counterexample by definition?
