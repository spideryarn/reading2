His route in goes through interoceptive inference: emotion and mood are perceptual best-guesses about the body's physiological state, and "this drive to stay alive doesn't bottom out anywhere in particular... reaches deep into the interior of each cell, into the molecular furnaces of metabolism" [spya-vys3vj].

**Tutor 2** — 77 words, 1 question mark, 2 citations, 3 article quotations, ⚠︎ no id in the sentence: “glimmers of an explanatory connection…”, flagged OWN VIEW

That's the mechanism he's pointing to — prediction-error minimization "becomes inseparable from the materiality of life itself" once you trace it into cellular metabolism [spya-vys3vj]. But notice he's careful about the strength of this: he calls it only "glimmers of an explanatory connection between life and consciousness" [spya-da9tvt], not a proof.

**Tutor 3** — 92 words, 1 question mark, 2 citations, 1 article quotation

That doubt sits right at the seam Seth himself flags — this is "non-computational, or at least non-algorithmic," because prediction-error minimization in real brains is "a continuous dynamical process that is likely inseparable from its material basis, rather than a meat-implemented algorithm" [spya-da9tvt]. His move isn't to deny you could abstract the dynamics mathematically, but to deny you could implement them without the actual self-sustaining metabolic substrate.

That's offered as evidence against assuming function transfers cleanly across substrates — what he calls generative entrenchment [spya-jnwfxp], not an assumption that it can't.

Does one documented case of substrate-dependent function generalize to license doubt about the dynamics in general, or does it only block the thought experiment's premise of *perfect* functional preservation?
