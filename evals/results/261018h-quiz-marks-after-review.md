**Question.** Why does Seth say brains are not computers?

**Draft answer.** Because inside a brain there is no sharp separation between 'mindware' and 'wetware' of the kind that exists between software and hardware in a computer. That separation is what the idea of the brain as a Turing machine depends on, so without it the case for computational functionalism weakens.

**Evidence.** `spya-b0086e` `spya-zw2m7u`

**The reader said:**

> honestly no idea. I lost the thread in that section. just tell me

**Verdict (hidden from the reader)** — `wrong` · matches the hand label

**Reply** — 135 words, 3 citations, 3.9s

Seth's main reason is that a brain has no clean split between what it does and what it is. In a computer, software (the program) is sharply separate from hardware (the machine running it). In a brain, he says, "there's no sharp separation between 'mindware' and 'wetware'" [spya-b0086e]. He also says that unlike computers, brains are things for which it is "difficult, and likely impossible, to separate what they do from what they are" [spya-zw2m7u].

This matters because the idea of the brain as a computer made of meat depends on that split, since a program should do the same thing whatever it runs on. If you can't separate the two in a brain, the brain-as-computer picture loses its footing, and so does the claim that the right computation alone is enough for consciousness [spya-p4vmm0].
