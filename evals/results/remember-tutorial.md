Seth's third argument — biological naturalism — claims life, not just computation, may be necessary for consciousness. He builds it from predictive processing: conscious states like emotion and mood are the brain's "perceptual best-guessing" about the body's own physiological condition, used for control and regulation [spya-b59nm2,spya-dgf4em]. He then pushes this down into metabolism itself, arguing prediction-error minimization "becomes inseparable from the materiality of life itself" at the cellular level [spya-vys3vj].

Given that predictive processing is usually formalized as a computational framework in your own field, where do you think Seth's move from "prediction error minimization" to "this must be non-algorithmic" actually happens?

> **Reader 2:** The link is meant to be that prediction error minimisation is bottomed out in metabolism, so the inference is tied to staying alive.

**Tutor 2** — 79 words, 1 question mark, 2 citations

That's the pivot — he grounds it in autopoiesis, arguing a line runs "directly from the self-producing, autopoietic nature of biological material all the way to the Bayesian best-guessing" [spya-vys3vj], then declares the result "non-computational, or at least non-algorithmic" because it's "a continuous dynamical process that is likely inseparable from its material basis" [spya-da9tvt].

Given that free energy principle work routinely implements prediction-error minimization in silico, where would you push back on his inference from "metabolically embedded" to "non-algorithmic"?
