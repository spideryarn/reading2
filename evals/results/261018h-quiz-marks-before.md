**Question.** Why does Seth say brains are not computers?

**Draft answer.** Because inside a brain there is no sharp separation between 'mindware' and 'wetware' of the kind that exists between software and hardware in a computer. That separation is what the idea of the brain as a Turing machine depends on, so without it the case for computational functionalism weakens.

**Evidence.** `spya-b0086e` `spya-zw2m7u`

**The reader said:**

> honestly no idea. I lost the thread in that section. just tell me

**Verdict (hidden from the reader)** — `wrong` · matches the hand label

**Reply** — 123 words, 3 citations, 3.9s

Seth's main reason is that a brain has no clean split between what it does and what it physically is. In a computer, software (the algorithm) is cleanly separate from hardware, but in a brain "there's no sharp separation between 'mindware' and 'wetware'" [spya-b0086e]. He adds that brains are things for which "it is difficult, and likely impossible, to separate what they do from what they are" [spya-zw2m7u].

This matters because the picture of the brain as a Turing machine (the standard mathematical model of a computer) "rests precisely on this sharp separation" [spya-p4vmm0]. If the brain's physical makeup matters to how it works, then computation alone isn't what counts, which undercuts the assumption that the right computation would be enough for consciousness.
