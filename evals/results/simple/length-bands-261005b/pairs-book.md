### Summary A

This long text looks for common rules behind the many designs of computer programs that learn. The authors say the field has many designs but few shared ideas, so the same ideas get reinvented. They borrow an old idea from geometry: study what stays the same when things change.

The key idea is symmetry: a change, like shifting a photo, that leaves what matters unchanged. Building known symmetries into these programs makes learning from complex data much easier. This approach, called Geometric Deep Learning, explains many popular designs and offers a guide for future ones.

### Summary B

This long text tries to bring order to deep learning, where computers learn from examples. There are many kinds of these learning programs, but few shared ideas link them. The authors call their unifying approach Geometric Deep Learning. It borrows an old idea from geometry: study what stays the same when things change.

This matters because learning from big, complex data like images can need impossibly many examples. Real data often has symmetry: changes that keep the meaning, like moving a cat within a photo. The authors show that many popular designs come from building in such symmetries. So one shared blueprint explains today's designs and offers a guide for building new ones.

### Summary B

### Summary A

This book is a collection of science fiction short stories by Cory Doctorow. It is also an experiment in a new way to publish and sell a book. It can be shared freely for non-money uses, with different prices for print and free online copies. The introduction says the stories matter because they warn about problems the internet may bring soon.

Many stories show people so excited by clever computer puzzles that they forget right from wrong. Some show computers watching everyone's data, like searches and email. The last story is about a computer mind that fights to avoid being switched off. The book ends with an essay saying most writers earn little, so new ways to pay them are needed.

### Summary B

"With a Little Help" is a book of short science fiction stories by Cory Doctorow. The stories imagine our near future online, like Google turning evil or a computer mind waking up. Several ask what happens when people chase clever tech puzzles and forget right and wrong.

The book is also an experiment in how to publish. Doctorow put it out himself under a Creative Commons license, which lets anyone share it free. Free downloads sit beside paid copies, to test how writers might earn money as book sales shrink.

### Summary A

This long text tries to bring order to deep learning, where computers learn from examples. There are many kinds of these learning programs, but few shared ideas link them. The authors call their unifying approach Geometric Deep Learning. It borrows an old idea from geometry: study what stays the same when things change.

This matters because learning from big, complex data like images can need impossibly many examples. Real data often has symmetry: changes that keep the meaning, like moving a cat within a photo. The authors show that many popular designs come from building in such symmetries. So one shared blueprint explains today's designs and offers a guide for building new ones.

### Summary B

The book walks through five kinds of data shape, including grids like images and networks of linked points.
