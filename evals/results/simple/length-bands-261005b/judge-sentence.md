# Judge: pairs-sentence

Judged from the headings and the two summaries only; anything under `bent:` is a suspicion, not a checked fact.

## P01

pad: both. A defines invariance twice ("invariance means a result does not move under that change" in paragraph 1, then "An invariant function gives the same answer when the input is transformed" in paragraph 4). B spends a sentence on "The authors use a simple geometric example of a bumpy function over a high-dimensional space to make this point concrete", which tells the reader nothing. Both are mild.
bent: A. Suspicion only. "convolution ... turns out to be the only linear operation that respects shifts" reads as an overclaim next to B's "follows directly from requiring shift symmetry". "the output should change no more than how far a distortion is from a true symmetry" is blurred to the point of being hard to parse. A names the two founding ideas as "symmetry and invariance" where B names "symmetry and scale separation", and the headings (3.4 Scale Separation as a prior of its own) sit closer to B. B has one loose step of its own: "Real tasks like vision or chemistry have long-range patterns, so the escape must come from the structure of the data itself" does not follow as written.
coverage: same. Both run sections 1 to 6 in order and neither mentions section 7 (the history). A gives recurrent networks their own paragraph; B gives limits their own paragraph and a second application. Neither is opening-heavy in the sense of dropping later parts, though both spend about half their length on sections 1 to 3.
prefer: B. Same ground, but its statements are plainer and it separates what the authors claim from the limits they admit.

## P03

Its last paragraph restates what earlier paragraphs said: "fits one scheme and comes from common geometric principles" repeats "Many popular models then appear as one blueprint", and "understand and design future architectures" repeats paragraph 2's "recipe for building future designs". B has one small filler, "future architectures not yet invented".

prefer: no. A reads more smoothly and has the limits paragraph; B has more of the middle of the book. They trade evenly.

## P04

pad: B. The one-drop rule is told twice (paragraph 2: "kept anyone with any African ancestry out of the 'white' group"; paragraph 6: "long counted anyone with one known Black ancestor as Black"), and paragraph 1 ends on a list of contents ("history, biology, several countries, several fields of study, and everyday uses"). A has a similar contents sentence but no repeat.
bent: B. Suspicion only. "Race is now widely seen as a pseudoscientific way of sorting people" reads as an overclaim beside the same summary's "Some researchers push back" and its account of race still being used in medicine. "societies make them up" / "Being made up" is stronger than "social construct" as either summary defines it. "A 2017 survey ... found agreement" is vague about how much agreement. The two also differ on Linnaeus's words for Africans ("crafty and lazy" in B, "lazy and careless" in A) and on forensics (B "ancestry", A "race").
coverage: B. B follows the headings nearly part for part: definition, origins, subspecies, clines, genetic clusters, the three countries, views by field, biomedicine, forensics. A is lopsided toward the genetics debate and surveys, and skips subspecies, the three countries and biomedicine although its opening promises law and medicine.
prefer: B. Much more of the article is laid out, in the article's own order; the two suspicious sentences are a price worth paying for an orientation.
