Main findings

1. Memory preserves salience, not fidelity: agents actively reinterpret and rewrite memories to suit a changing self and world ("mnemonic improvisation"), and confabulation is a feature, not a bug.
2. The persistence paradox (change and you cease to be, do not change and you die out or cannot learn) holds at every scale and bears on engineered minds; the answer is the Self as a process, not a thing.
3. Memories are messages between brief "Selflets" separated in time; an engram is a note from a past self that must be interpreted, more a prompt than a recording.
4. Memories are remapped onto new bodies and contexts, not merely stored: caterpillar-to-butterfly memories, memory transfer by brain extract, the odorant injected into a frog egg.
5. The same holds beyond the brain through a bowtie (compression then creative re-expansion) architecture: the egg, context-read bioelectric triggers, polyploid newts reaching the same anatomy by different means.
6. The unreliability of the biological substrate is the source of intelligence, not a limit on it: planaria with the noisiest genome regenerate best, species differ in willingness to confabulate in anatomical space, and this drives an intelligence ratchet.
7. Speculatively, the line between thoughts and thinkers blurs: memories may have minimal agency and may act as cognitive glue that helps make the Self real.
8. The Self is a construction, an adaptive story that holds parts together and must be continually repaired; the author says he has stated the strongest version and a more moderate one may prove more useful.
