Main findings

1. The Matched Filter Hypothesis: prefrontal cognitive control works as a filter on low-level information, and performance is best when the level of filtering matches what the task needs, not simply when control is high.
2. The task split: high control suits tasks that are explicit, rule-based, verbal or abstract and fit within working memory; low control (hypofrontality) suits tasks that are implicit, reward-based or stimulus-driven, non-verbal or intuitive, and exceed working memory.
3. The level of control reached comes from competition between prefrontal cortex and posterior or subcortical systems, and is moderated by individual differences: developmental stage, genes, brain damage, dual-task conditions, brain stimulation.
4. Learning evidence: reduced control (a second task, magnetic stimulation, childhood) can improve learning, as in implicit or procedural learning, retrieval-induced forgetting, blocking and highlighting, and language learning from noisy input; control buys fast error reduction at a long-term cost to the fidelity and completeness of what is learned.
5. Competing learning systems: a prefrontal/caudate declarative, goal-directed system against a striatal procedural, habitual one, with the mature prefrontal cortex biasing learners to the declarative system even when it suits the problem poorly.
6. Creativity evidence: generating ideas in open-ended, data-driven tasks benefits from lower prefrontal activity (imaging, inhibitory stimulation of left prefrontal cortex, children's resistance to functional fixedness), while evaluating an idea likely needs prefrontal control again.
7. The stated limits: direct evidence is sparse and creativity studies few and sometimes inconsistent; children's advantages cannot be attributed to hypofrontality alone; less prefrontal function does not linearly help (reversal learning, inverted-U with dopamine); the neural mechanism of matching is unspecified.
8. The conclusion: cognitive control is a tool adapted to a subset of common challenges, not an all-purpose optimisation system.
